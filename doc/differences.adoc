[#trystero-differences]
== Differences from Trystero

trystero ports the part of Trystero 0.26.0 that rooms of messages need: the
Nostr strategy, the signaling, the password handshake, the action wire and
the room's API. Where it keeps a feature, it keeps Trystero's wire and its
defaults; where it bounds what a peer or a relay can make a room hold, it
says so in <<trystero-protocol-bounds>>, since Trystero JS bounds none of it.
These features of Trystero are not ported, each for its reason:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Not ported | Why

| Audio and video: `addStream`, `removeStream`, `addTrack`, `removeTrack`,
  `replaceTrack`, `onPeerStream` and `onPeerTrack`, with the actions
  `@_stream`, `@_track` and `@_signal` and their renegotiation
| The largest part left, on the roadmap
  (<<trystero-roadmap>>): in the browser, the page's `MediaStream` objects;
  natively, libdatachannel's RTP tracks, whose capture, encoding and
  decoding would be the application's. A room ignores a frame of one of
  these actions.

| Request actions, `kind: 'request'`: `request`, `requestMany` and
  `onRequest`
| Not ported yet: an application answers a message with another, to its
  sender's id.

| `onReceive`, which accepts or declines an incoming transfer; the progress
  callbacks `onProgress` and `onReceiveProgress`; and cancelling a send with
  an `AbortSignal`
| Not ported yet: a room accepts every transfer for an action with a
  receiver within `max_receive_bytes`, as Trystero does when no `onReceive`
  is set, and reports a transfer only when it completes.

| `onPeerHandshake`, a handshake of the application's own
| Not ported yet: the password handshake, which runs on the same internal
  action, `@_hsdata`, is.

| Passive rooms, `passive: true`
| Not ported yet: a room always announces itself.

| The BitTorrent, MQTT, Firebase, Supabase, IPFS and WebSocket relay
  strategies
| Each is a strategy of its own to port; Nostr's public relays need no
  account and no server of the application's own.

| `getRelaySockets`, `pauseRelayReconnection`, `resumeRelayReconnection`,
  `manualReconnection` and `warnOnRelayFailure`
| Not ported yet: a room reconnects with its backoff, and reports what its
  relays do through its log.

| `trickleIce: false`, `turnConfig` and `rtcPolyfill`
| Candidates are always trickled, as Trystero's Nostr strategy does by
  default; `ice_servers` names every STUN and TURN server, in place of
  Trystero's defaults; and the backend is what a polyfill would be.
|===

A feature not ported has no program among the differences below: trystero
has nothing of it to run beside its twin.

[#trystero-differences-behaviour]
=== Where a ported feature behaves otherwise

[cols="2,3,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Behaviour | Trystero JS | Here | Why

| Scope of sharing and of the self id | the page: one `selfId` for every app and room, one manager per strategy | an `app` object: its self id and its connections | no static state; several peers in one process; an id across apps is a program's choice (`app_config::self_id`) and never reaches the wire of another app

| Passwords across rooms | independently configured per room, including mixed passwords on one shared connection | one immutable `app_config::password` for every room, separate apps and self ids for different passwords | the initial sealed negotiation authenticates one password domain before any of its rooms reuse the connection; the JS wire remains unchanged

| A peer kicked from a room, or that kicked us, over a live connection | bound again by its next signal or presence | bound again at a rebinding time, 11 s doubling to 5 min; the lower id refuses it meanwhile and keeps its frames | a peer sending invalid room proofs would otherwise loop reports and announcements without end; one refusing side cannot alternate with another

| Reuse over a live connection | binds on any signal of that peer id | binds only a peer that advertised the room's token itself, and by reuse only on their first meeting over that connection | a forged announcement must not bind an innocent peer

| Announcements after a peer leaves | restarted after every leave | restarted only when no live connection to that peer remains | over a live connection, rebinding goes through the connection

| Presence after binding | sent when a room registers | also sent again when a room binds by reuse or at its rebinding time | it makes the other side, Trystero JS included, bind at once

| An answer to an earlier challenge | checked against the current challenge only | accepted when it is correct for any of the last 8 challenges sent since the last completed handshake | a late answer of an ended binding is honest and proves the password; failing it reports a false error

| Frames after the higher id's own kick | kept for the next binding | dropped until the higher id sends presence | they belong to the ended binding; flushed into the next one, they would answer or be answered in its place

| Joining a room already joined | the same room | `error::already_joined` | a `room` is a handle whose destruction leaves

| Rooms per app | unbounded; a peer advertising a 65th is dropped by the other side | `join` refuses the 65th, counting rooms in their farewell | the 65th would cost every connection of the app

| A connection whose last room was kicked | idle 123,333 ms | closed at once | a bound: a failed peer holds no connection

| Relay sockets and the Nostr key | shared by the rooms of the page, topics batched per socket | per room | not part of this decision; invisible to peers; shared through the app in a later change

| Relay and ICE configuration | the first room's relays; each negotiating room's RTC configuration | the app's, for every room | one configuration for what the rooms share

| `idle_timeout` | test-only (`_test_only_sharedPeerIdleMs`) | a configuration member, Trystero's default | tests and programs that close sooner

| Frames waiting for a bound room's handler | 64, 10 s, then the connection drops | no such state | a binding's delivery is set when it is made

| Renegotiation and media over a shared connection | routed through a control room, streams owned per room; a connection with any media is destroyed when its last room goes | not ported | media is on the roadmap (<<trystero-roadmap>>)

| Passive rooms | presence only for active rooms | not ported | not ported yet: a room always announces itself

| Crossed duplicate connections | first registered wins at each side; crossed, both lose and meet again | the same | matching Trystero's rule is what keeps a {cpp} and a Trystero JS side agreeing

| A Nostr event a relay repeats, or that several relays carry | acted on each time | acted on once among the 4096 newest event ids, when its id is checked to be its own, the SHA-256 of its NIP-01 serialization; an event with any other id is acted on each time and its id never kept | every relay of the room carries each event; an id taken unchecked would let a relay hide a genuine event behind a forged one it sends first under the same id, or fill the room's resource with the ids kept

| A relay's text, a signal or a handshake message past its bound | parsed with `JSON.parse` whatever its length, under the relay's own limit | dropped before it is read: 68,608 bytes for a relay's text, 66,560 for a signal, 1,024 for a handshake message (<<trystero-protocol-json>>) | the bounds are those of the fields Trystero sends, so no text a Trystero peer writes is refused; a longer one would cost the room its length to read

| A protocol text of thousands of values | parsed | refused once its image passes twice its bound and 64 KiB | no Trystero message holds more than a few dozen values, and a document's value takes some 200 bytes of image

| A self id that is not UTF-8 | impossible: a JavaScript string | set aside for a random one, with a configuration report | JSON carries UTF-8 alone, and a signal writes the id as a JSON string

| A JSON payload's number that a double cannot hold: an integer past 2^53^, a decimal of more digits, one past a double's range | sent as the nearest double, and one past a double's range as `null`: a JavaScript number is a double | sent exact, as webcpp::document holds it; a Trystero JS peer reads the nearest double, or `Infinity` | webcpp::document keeps every number exact, and writes each one a double holds as JavaScript does

| A JSON payload nested deeper than 256, or holding a lone surrogate | delivered: `JSON.parse` takes both | not delivered | webcpp::document's defaults: a bounded depth, and strings that are UTF-8

| A JSON payload whose document needs more than twice its length and 64 KiB of image: an array of more than 345 zeros, 352 numbers of three digits, 287 short strings or 101 objects of two members | delivered: `JSON.parse` takes any | not delivered (<<trystero-differences-json-refused>>) | each value of a document is a node of some 200 bytes of image, so the image's bound is tied to the payload's length, as a protocol text's to its bound (<<trystero-protocol-json>>), and a payload of many small values costs the room no more than a multiple of its length

| The characters of a random id (the self id, offer ids, nonces, subscription ids) | each of the 62 alike, from `Math.random` | one random byte each, at its remainder in 62: each of the first eight characters from 5 of the 256 bytes, each other from 4, about 5.95 bits a character | the bytes come from the backend's secure randomness, not `Math.random`; the bias costs about 0.005 bits a character, below log2(62)

|===

[#trystero-differences-already-joined]
==== Joining a room already joined

Trystero JS:: `joinRoom` with the id of a room the page holds returns that
room.
trystero:: `app::join` refuses with `error::already_joined` while a room of
that id is joined.
Why:: A `room` is a handle whose destruction leaves the room: two handles of
one room would leave it twice.

[#trystero-differences-rooms]
==== Rooms per app

Trystero JS:: `joinRoom` joins every room a page asks for: the 65th, 66th and
67th rooms of one `appId` are each a room. It advertises every room of the
app over every connection of the app, and a peer that is advertised a 65th
room token over one connection drops that connection, "too many advertised
rooms" (`dist/shared-peer.mjs:457`).
trystero:: `app::join` refuses a 65th room with `error::too_many_rooms`. A
room still in its farewell, the 99 ms of `async_leave`, counts, so another
room can be joined once that leave has completed.
Why:: A 65th room would be advertised over every connection of the app, and
each Trystero JS peer at the other end would drop the connection.

[#trystero-differences-self-id]
==== The self id

Trystero JS:: A page has one `selfId`, drawn once when Trystero's module
loads, and every room of every app announces it, so a peer that meets the
page in two apps sees the same id in both. Its one manager of shared
connections keys them by app id, so the two apps share the id and not their
connections.
trystero:: Each app draws its own self id and holds its own connections, so a
peer that meets the program in rooms of two apps sees two ids, and two rooms
of one app show one. A program that wants one id across apps names it,
`app_config::self_id`.
Why:: No static state, so that one process holds several peers; an id shared
across apps is the program's choice, and never reaches another app's wire
unless the program chooses it.

[#trystero-differences-passwords]
==== Passwords across rooms

Trystero JS:: Each `joinRoom` takes its own password, so one page joins a
lobby with one password and a kitchen with another, and a peer that joins
both meets the page as one peer, over one connection that carries both
rooms' password handshakes.
trystero:: A password is an app's, `app_config::password`, the same for every
room of the app and never changed: rooms of different passwords are rooms of
separate apps, each with its own self id and connections, so the other peer
meets two peers.
Why:: The sealed negotiation that makes a connection authenticates one
password before any room of the app reuses it; the wire stays Trystero's.

[#trystero-differences-relay-config]
==== Relay and ICE configuration

Trystero JS:: The first room a page joins, while it holds no other, opens the
relays, and every room joined while one remains uses them, whatever relays
it was given. A connection is made with the RTC configuration of the room
that negotiates it, and a room that binds over it later shares it. So a
kitchen joined with a second configuration after the lobby uses the lobby's
relays and the lobby's ICE servers.
trystero:: Relays and ICE servers are the app's, for every room of the app: a
room with another configuration is a room of another app, which uses its
own. A room hands out neither its relays' sockets nor its connections, so
the program gives each app a backend that records what the app asks of the
platform's.
Why:: One configuration for what the rooms of an app share.

[#trystero-differences-peer-join]
==== `on_peer_join` does not replay the joined peers

Trystero JS:: A newly set `onPeerJoin` is called at once with each peer
already joined.
trystero:: A room reports a join from its executor, after the call to `join`
has returned, to the handler set then, so a handler set right after `join`
sees every join; `peers()` lists the joined peers for a handler set later.
Why:: A join is an event, reported once; the peers joined are the room's
state, which `peers()` reads.

[#trystero-differences-json-numbers]
==== A JSON payload's numbers that a double cannot hold

Trystero JS:: A JSON payload is sent as `JSON.stringify` writes a JavaScript
value, whose numbers are doubles: `JSON.parse` reads an integer past 2^53^,
or a decimal of more digits than a double keeps, as the nearest double, and a
number past a double's range as `Infinity`, which `JSON.stringify` writes as
`null`.
trystero:: A `webcpp::document::document` holds each number exactly, and a room
sends it as the document writes it, each number a double holds as JavaScript
writes it. A Trystero JS peer that receives the payload reads the nearest
double, or `Infinity`.
Why:: webcpp::document keeps every number exact, and writes each one a double
holds as JavaScript does.

[#trystero-differences-json-refused]
==== A JSON payload nested deeper than 256, holding a lone surrogate, or of many small values

Trystero JS:: `JSON.parse` reads a payload nested deeper than 256, a string
that holds a lone surrogate, `"\ud800"`, as `JSON.stringify` writes one, and
an array of 1,000 zeros, so a room delivers all three.
trystero:: A room reads a JSON payload with webcpp::document's defaults, into
an image of at most twice the payload and 64 KiB, and delivers none: a text
nested deeper than 256 is refused, and so is a lone surrogate, and an array
of 1,000 zeros, whose nodes would need some 190 KB. The room goes on, and
delivers the payloads sent after them.
Why:: webcpp::document's defaults: a bounded depth, and strings that are
UTF-8; and each value of a document is a node of some 200 bytes of image, so
the image's bound is tied to the payload's length, as a protocol text's to
its bound (<<trystero-protocol-json>>), and a payload of many small values
costs the room no more than a multiple of its length.

A Trystero JS page sends such payloads, and no {cpp} peer sends a lone
surrogate, which a document's UTF-8 strings cannot hold. So the program's
other peer sends what a page sends: each payload's JSON text, as
`JSON.stringify` writes the value, as a text payload, which the backend its
app is given marks as JSON on the wire.

[#trystero-differences-unshown]
=== The differences no program shows

These rows of <<trystero-differences-behaviour>> have no program, each for
its reason.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Behaviour | Why no program shows it

| A peer kicked from a room, or that kicked us, over a live connection; frames after the higher id's own kick; a connection whose last room was kicked
| A kick follows a room proof that fails over a live connection, and two {cpp} apps never send one: an app has one password for every room, and apps of different passwords share no connection. A Trystero JS page that mixes passwords does, so the interop lane's wrong room proofs show trystero's side, against a page and not a twin (<<trystero-testing-lanes>>), and the `shared` test holds the rest over the memory relays.

| Reuse over a live connection
| Only a forged announcement shows it: a third party that publishes, in a peer's name, a room token that peer never advertised, which neither API publishes. The `shared` test forges one over the memory relays.

| Announcements after a peer leaves
| What differs is what a room publishes to its relays after a peer leaves it while their connection stays, which neither API reports: the peers see the same joins and leaves.

| Presence after binding
| A frame on the connection, which neither API reports; what it changes is how soon the other side binds, a time that no two runs print alike.

| An answer to an earlier challenge
| It needs a handshake answer that arrives after a newer challenge, a race between two bindings that no program sets up on a real network; the `shared` test holds it over the memory relays, whose frames it holds and releases.

| Relay sockets and the Nostr key: the key
| Which key signs a room's events only the relay sees; neither a room nor a peer reports it. The sockets are shown (<<trystero-differences-relay-sockets>>).

| `idle_timeout`
| Not a behaviour: both keep an idle connection 123,333 ms, and Trystero JS takes the same setting under its test-only name, so a program and its twin would agree.

| Frames waiting for a bound room's handler
| A state Trystero JS passes through between a binding and its room's data handler, which its rooms set as they bind; no program reaches it through the API.

| Renegotiation and media over a shared connection; passive rooms
| Not ported: trystero has nothing of them to run.

| Crossed duplicate connections
| Not a difference: both keep the connection registered first.

| A Nostr event a relay repeats, or that several relays carry
| What an event changes when it comes twice is the signaling's work, offers and answers made again, which neither API reports: a program sees one join either way, and over honest relays an event's id is always its own. The `protocol` and `room` tests hold the dedup, forged ids included.

| A relay's text, a signal or a handshake message past its bound; a protocol text of thousands of values
| No Trystero peer writes such a text, so only a relay or a peer that means harm sends one, and what changes is whether the room reads it, which neither API reports. The `protocol` test holds the bounds.

| The characters of a random id
| A bias of about 0.005 bits a character, which only a count over many ids shows, and a count of random characters prints something else on every run.
|===
