[#trystero-differences]
== Differences from Trystero

On the branch `port/trystero-0.27`, not interoperable before Phase 2.
trystero ports the part of Trystero 0.27.0 that rooms of messages need: the
Nostr strategy, the signaling, the action wire with its request actions, and
the room's API with its listeners, users and errors. Where it keeps a
feature, it keeps Trystero's wire and its defaults; where it bounds what a
peer or a relay can make a room hold, it says so in
<<trystero-protocol-bounds>>, since Trystero JS bounds none of it. Phase 1 of
the port keeps two parts of 0.26.0 that 0.27.0 replaced with ECDSA P-256:
the connection an app's rooms share, with its presence frames and password
handshake, and a peer id drawn at random (<<trystero-design-phase-1>>);
<<trystero-differences-shared>> compares the first with 0.26.0's, since
0.27.0 has none. These features of Trystero are not ported, each for its
reason:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Not ported | Why

| Audio and video: `addStream`, `removeStream`, `replaceTrack` and
  `onPeerStream`, with the actions `@_stream` and `@_signal` and their
  renegotiation
| The largest part left, on the roadmap
  (<<trystero-roadmap>>): in the browser, the page's `MediaStream` objects;
  natively, libdatachannel's RTP tracks, whose capture, encoding and
  decoding would be the application's. A room ignores a frame of one of
  these actions.

| Identities: the `identity` option, `createUserIdentity`,
  `exportUserIdentity`, `importUserIdentity` and `clearUserIdentity`, the
  signed handshake, `maxUsers`
| Phase 2: each needs ECDSA P-256, which webcpp::crypto's `curves::p256`
  brings. Until then a peer's id is drawn at random, its user's id is its
  own, and `room_full`, `banned`, `identity_failed`, `handshake_failed` and
  `peer_error` are reported by nothing. 0.27.0's default, `identity:
  'local'`, keeps the user key in IndexedDB; the port will have no implicit
  persistence (design D15): a program keeps an identity with
  `export_user_identity` and `import_user_identity`.

| `makeState`, `makeUserState`, `roles`, `scale` and the plugins of `use`,
  with `ready`
| Phase 3, a change of its own each, after the port merges.

| `requestMany`; a send's and a request's `onProgress` and `signal`; a
  listener's `signal`
| Not ported yet; a listener's subscription replaces its `signal`
  (<<trystero-usage-listeners>>).

| Passive rooms, `passive: true`
| Not ported yet: a room always announces itself.

| The BitTorrent, MQTT, Firebase, Supabase, IPFS and WebSocket relay
  strategies
| Each is a strategy of its own to port (design D3); Nostr's public relays
  need no account and no server of the application's own.

| `getRelaySockets` and `warnOnRelayFailure`
| A room reconnects with its backoff, and reports what its relays do through
  its log and `relay_failed`.

| `trickleIce: false`, `rtcConfig`, `turnConfig` and `rtcPolyfill`
| Candidates are always trickled, as Trystero's Nostr strategy does by
  default; `ice_servers` names every STUN and TURN server, in place of
  Trystero's defaults; and the backend is what a polyfill would be.
|===

A feature not ported has no program among the differences below: trystero
has nothing of it to run beside its twin.

[#trystero-differences-behaviour]
=== Where a ported feature behaves otherwise

[cols="2,3,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Behaviour | Trystero JS | Here | Why

| Scope of the peer id | the page: one peer id for each app id, from an endpoint identity the page creates once and caches, and a user id from the user's key | an `app` object: its peer id, drawn for it, and its user's id the same, until Phase 2 | no static state; several peers in one process; P-256 keys come with Phase 2

| Passwords across rooms | configured per room | one immutable `app_config::password` for every room, separate apps and peer ids for different passwords | the shared connection's sealed negotiation authenticates one password domain before any of its rooms reuse it

| Rooms per app | unbounded | `join` refuses the 65th, counting rooms in their farewell | the shared connection advertises each room's token, and a 65th would cost every connection of the app

| Relay sockets and the Nostr key | one socket per relay URL for the page, shared by the rooms that name it; a signing key per room | a socket per room and relay; a signing key per room | invisible to peers; sockets shared through the app in a later change

| Relay and ICE configuration | each room's own | the app's, for every room | one configuration for what the rooms share

| Listener tokens | `onPeerJoin` and the others return a function that removes the listener, and take a `signal` | they return a `subscription`, whose `unsubscribe()` removes the listener and whose destruction leaves it; no `signal` | a token that removed its listener when destroyed would silently remove the listeners whose token a program did not keep (design D10)

| `getSelf` | a promise, since WebCrypto makes the keys | `self()`, at once | an app's identity exists once it is made (design D11)

| `getPeers` | each peer's `PeerInfo` with its `connection` | `peer_info` without it | the connection is the backend's (design D11)

| `getPeers`, `getUsers` and a user's `peerIds` | objects and arrays in the order of insertion: a user's peers in the order their entries were made | a `std::pmr::map` of each in id order, with a transparent comparator; a user's peers in the order they became active | a map {cpp} looks a peer up in by `std::string_view` without building a string; the order of activity is the order a program sees joins

| A plaintext that is not UTF-8 | opened with `TextDecoder`, which replaces each invalid sequence by U+FFFD | refused as `decrypt_failed` | an SDP is ASCII, and a replaced text would be another text than the peer sealed (design D12)

| Relay connectivity | `setRelayConnectivity`, for the whole page; `'auto'` follows the page's `online`, `offline` and `visibilitychange`, reconnecting when the page becomes visible and is not offline; a value outside the three throws | `app::set_relay_connectivity`, per app; `automatic` follows `online` and `offline` alone, and natively is online; a value outside the enum, which only a cast makes, is taken as online | a process may hold several independent apps (design D13), and the design names `online` and `offline` alone

| A send to a target that has not joined | warns on the console and resolves | completes with `error::not_connected` | an operation that fails says so with its error code

| `onReceive` and `onRequest` | may answer with a promise: at most 128 receive decisions pending, 8 of one peer, and 64 concurrent requests of one peer; a gate set after an offer arrived is asked at the offer's next drain | answer at once, during the frame's processing, so none of those bounds has a counterpart; a gate set after an offer arrived is not asked of it | a callable that answers at once is the {cpp} form of a decision the frame waits for; an asynchronous one is a later change

| A refused request | rejects with the reason the peer gave | completes with `error::request_rejected` alone, the reason in the room's log | an error code carries no text of a peer's

| An `@_response` that answers no request | its offer rejected, "unexpected response" | dropped, inline, or received and dropped, as a transfer | nothing waits for it

| A name made both a message action and a request action | throws "action type cannot be redefined" | the second handle's operations complete with `error::action_redefined` | trystero raises no exception of its own

| A JSON payload or metadata holding a lone surrogate | delivered: `JSON.parse` takes it | not delivered; the reason of a refused request, which 0.27.0 cuts at 512 UTF-16 code units and may leave ending in one, is read with U+FFFD in its place | webcpp::document's strings are UTF-8

| The hint of `peer_connect_failed` without TURN, and `turnConfigured` | "configure TURN via turnConfig or rtcConfig.iceServers"; a TURN server of the raw configuration counts | "configure TURN via app_config::ice_servers"; only a TURN server the app kept counts | the port's option is `ice_servers`, and a server it dropped as unusable is none

| `relay-failed` | reported when every relay's first connection failed before one opened, or when every initial announcement failed | the first only | the strategy skips an announcement a relay cannot take now, rather than failing it

| A Nostr event a relay repeats, or that several relays carry | acted on each time | acted on once among the 4096 newest event ids, when its id is checked to be its own, the SHA-256 of its NIP-01 serialization; an event with any other id is acted on each time and its id never kept | every relay of the room carries each event; an id taken unchecked would let a relay hide a genuine event behind a forged one it sends first under the same id, or fill the room's resource with the ids kept

| A relay's text, a signal or a handshake message past its bound | parsed with `JSON.parse` whatever its length, under the relay's own limit | dropped before it is read: 68,608 bytes for a relay's text, 66,560 for a signal, 1,024 for a handshake message (<<trystero-protocol-json>>) | the bounds are those of the fields Trystero sends, so no text a Trystero peer writes is refused; a longer one would cost the room its length to read

| A protocol text of thousands of values | parsed | refused once its image passes twice its bound and 64 KiB | no Trystero message holds more than a few dozen values, and a document's value takes some 200 bytes of image

| A protocol text nested deeper than 16 | parsed | dropped as a malformed one is | no Trystero message nests deeper than 4, a relay's event and its tags, and each level of a parse is a level of recursion, which a hostile relay would otherwise take to 256

| A JSON payload's number that a double cannot hold: an integer past 2^53^, a decimal of more digits, one past a double's range | sent as the nearest double, and one past a double's range as `null`: a JavaScript number is a double | sent exact, as webcpp::document holds it; a Trystero JS peer reads the nearest double, or `Infinity` | webcpp::document keeps every number exact, and writes each one a double holds as JavaScript does

| A JSON payload nested deeper than 256 | delivered: `JSON.parse` takes it | not delivered | webcpp::document's default, a bounded depth

| A JSON payload whose document passes the room's `room_options::max_json_document_bytes`, 64 MiB by default: some 350,000 values of one character | delivered: `JSON.parse` has no bound but the page's memory | not delivered (<<trystero-differences-json-refused>>) | each value of a document is a node of 192 bytes of image, 96 times its text at most, so a program bounds what a peer's JSON may take of its room's resource; every payload within the bound is delivered
|===

[#trystero-differences-shared]
=== The connection an app's rooms share, kept from 0.26.0

0.27.0 negotiates one connection per room and remote peer, and admits a
peer by a handshake that both sides sign; it has none of what follows. On
the branch an app's rooms still share 0.26.0's connection to each remote
peer, until Phase 2's first task reads 0.27.0's connection model and
retires it (design D9), so these rows compare that sharing with 0.26.0's
`shared-peer.ts`, whose frames the branch still writes inside a room's
connection.

[cols="2,3,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Behaviour | Trystero JS 0.26.0 | Here | Why

| A peer kicked from a room, or that kicked us, over a live connection | bound again by its next signal or presence | bound again at a rebinding time, 11 s doubling to 5 min; the lower id refuses it meanwhile and keeps its frames | a peer sending invalid room proofs would otherwise loop reports and announcements without end; one refusing side cannot alternate with another

| Reuse over a live connection | binds on any signal of that peer id | binds only a peer that advertised the room's token itself, and by reuse only on their first meeting over that connection | a forged announcement must not bind an innocent peer

| A room frame before the peer's presence for the room | kept until the room binds by presence or by reuse | also taken as the peer's advertisement of the room, which binds it | a forged announcement cannot reuse, so a presence that Chrome dropped as the page's channel opened would otherwise leave the room unbound for good; only the peer writes its connection's frames

| Announcements after a peer leaves | restarted after every leave | restarted only when no live connection to that peer remains | over a live connection, rebinding goes through the connection

| Presence after binding | sent when a room registers | also sent again when a room binds by reuse or at its rebinding time | it makes the other side bind at once

| An answer to an earlier challenge | checked against the current challenge only | accepted when it is correct for any of the last 8 challenges sent since the last completed handshake | a late answer of an ended binding is honest and proves the password; failing it reports a false error

| Frames after the higher id's own kick | kept for the next binding | dropped until the higher id sends presence | they belong to the ended binding; flushed into the next one, they would answer or be answered in its place

| A connection whose last room was kicked | idle 123,333 ms | closed at once | a bound: a failed peer holds no connection

| `idle_timeout` | test-only (`_test_only_sharedPeerIdleMs`) | a configuration member, Trystero's default | tests and programs that close sooner

| Frames waiting for a bound room's handler | 64, 10 s, then the connection drops | no such state | a binding's delivery is set when it is made

| Renegotiation and media over a shared connection | routed through a control room, streams owned per room; a connection with any media is destroyed when its last room goes | not ported | media is on the roadmap (<<trystero-roadmap>>)

| Duplicate connections | the first registered wins at each side and the other is closed at once; crossed, both lose and meet again | the first registered wins at each side; the other is held, unused, until the peer shows which it keeps: closed once the peer sends on the first, or after 2 s (higher id) or 10 s (lower id) when the peer does neither; it replaces the first when the peer closes the first, or, for the higher id, when the peer sends on it | libdatachannel ends an association with a stream reset and a SHUTDOWN, its ABORT a moment later, where a browser's ABORT follows its reset at once: closing at once the connection a Trystero JS peer kept, as it starts sending on it, made Chrome fail those sends and Trystero JS report a peer error. Following the peer's choice closes no connection the peer uses, keeps a crossed pair's rooms on the connection the peer kept, and still agrees with Trystero's rule
|===

[#trystero-differences-rooms]
==== Rooms per app

Trystero JS:: `joinRoom` joins every room a page asks for: the 65th, 66th and
67th rooms of one `appId` are each a room, each with connections of its own.
0.26.0 joined them too, and advertised every room of the app over every
connection the app's rooms shared, so that a peer advertised a 65th room
token over one connection dropped that connection, "too many advertised
rooms" (`dist/shared-peer.mjs:457`).
trystero:: `app::join` refuses a 65th room with `error::too_many_rooms`. A
room still in its farewell, the 99 ms of `async_leave`, counts, so another
room can be joined once that leave has completed.
Why:: On the branch an app's rooms still share 0.26.0's connection
(<<trystero-design-phase-1>>), which advertises each room's token: a 65th
would be advertised over every connection of the app. Phase 2 retires the
sharing, and this difference with it.

[#trystero-differences-passwords]
==== Passwords across rooms

Trystero JS:: Each `joinRoom` takes its own password, so one page joins a
lobby with one password and a kitchen with another, and a peer that joins
both meets the page as one peer, its one peer id, over a connection of each
room; 0.26.0 carried both rooms' password handshakes over one connection.
trystero:: A password is an app's, `app_config::password`, the same for every
room of the app and never changed: rooms of different passwords are rooms of
separate apps, each with its own self id and connections, so the other peer
meets two peers.
Why:: The sealed negotiation that makes a shared connection authenticates
one password before any room of the app reuses it (0.1.0's ruling, kept by
design D6, so that a room's keys are cached by room id alone).

[#trystero-differences-relay-config]
==== Relay and ICE configuration

Trystero JS:: Each `joinRoom` takes its own `relayConfig` and `rtcConfig`: a
kitchen joined with a second configuration after the lobby uses the second
configuration's relays and ICE servers, under the page's one peer id. 0.26.0
gave every room joined while one remained the first room's relays, and a
room that bound over a shared connection its negotiating room's ICE
servers, as its twin printed.
trystero:: Relays and ICE servers are the app's, for every room of the app: a
room with another configuration is a room of another app, which uses its
own, under another peer id. A room hands out neither its relays' sockets nor
its connections, so the program gives each app a backend that records what
the app asks of the platform's.
Why:: One configuration for what the rooms of an app share, its relays and
its shared connections.

[#trystero-differences-peers]
==== `peers()` has no connection

Trystero JS:: `getPeers` returns an object that maps each active peer's id to
its `PeerInfo`: its user's id, whether it is direct, its hops, its roles and
its `connection`, an `RTCPeerConnection` of the class given as
`rtcPolyfill` when one is given. 0.26.0 mapped each id to its connection.
trystero:: `peers()` maps each active peer's id to its `peer_info`, the same
members without the connection.
Why:: A connection is the backend's, webcpp::webrtc's over a page's object in
the browser and over libdatachannel's natively, shared by the app's rooms,
and the room does not hand it out.

[#trystero-differences-json-numbers]
==== A JSON payload's numbers that a double cannot hold

Trystero JS:: A JSON payload is sent as `JSON.stringify` writes a JavaScript
value, whose numbers are doubles: `JSON.parse` reads an integer past 2^53^,
or a decimal of more digits than a double keeps, as the nearest double, and a
number past a double's range as `Infinity`, which `JSON.stringify` writes as
`null`.
trystero:: A `webcpp::document::document` holds each number exactly, and a room
sends it as the document writes it, each number a double holds as JavaScript
writes it. A Trystero JS peer that receives the payload reads the nearest
double, or `Infinity`.
Why:: webcpp::document keeps every number exact, and writes each one a double
holds as JavaScript does.

[#trystero-differences-json-refused]
==== A JSON payload nested deeper than 256, holding a lone surrogate, or past the room's bound

Trystero JS:: `JSON.parse` reads a payload nested deeper than 256, a string
that holds a lone surrogate, `"\ud800"`, as `JSON.stringify` writes one, and
an array of any length, so a room delivers all three.
trystero:: A room reads a JSON payload with webcpp::document's defaults, into
an image of at most `room_options::max_json_document_bytes`, 64 MiB by
default, and delivers none: a text nested deeper than 256 is refused, and so
is a lone surrogate, and an array of 1,000 zeros, whose nodes need some
190 KB, in a room whose bound is 64 KiB. The room goes on, and delivers the
payloads sent after them.
Why:: webcpp::document's defaults: a bounded depth, and strings that are
UTF-8; and each value of a document is a node of 192 bytes of image, 96
times its text at most, so a program bounds what a peer's JSON may take of
its room's resource. Every payload within the bound is delivered: its image
is first bounded by 96 times its length and 64 KiB, which every valid text
fits (<<trystero-protocol-json>>).

[#trystero-differences-unshown]
=== The differences no program shows

These rows of <<trystero-differences-behaviour>> and
<<trystero-differences-shared>> have no program, each for its reason.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Behaviour | Why no program shows it

| Scope of the peer id
| Both sides draw their peer ids, so a program and its twin print ids that no two runs share, and neither API lets a program name one: what differs is which object holds an id and its connections, which the `app_impl` and `shared` tests hold over the memory relays.

| Listener tokens, `getSelf`, the order of `getPeers` and `getUsers`, `onReceive` and `onRequest`, a refused request, a redefined action
| What differs is the form of the API, a token, a value at once, a map, a callable that answers at once, an error code, which a program and its twin write each in its own language; the `subscription`, `room` and `user_table` tests hold the {cpp} side over the memory relays. Each program and twin run again once Phase 2 makes the rooms meet Trystero 0.27.0.

| A plaintext that is not UTF-8; an `@_response` that answers no request; a JSON metadata holding a lone surrogate
| Only a peer that means harm, or one that writes a text no Trystero peer writes, sends one. The `crypto_layer` test refuses such a plaintext, and the `room` test reads a refusal's reason that ends in a lone surrogate.

| Relay connectivity; `relay-failed`
| What differs is a page's event, `visibilitychange`, which no native program and twin receive, and an announcement every relay refuses, which no local relay of the lanes refuses; the `room` and `platform_page_connectivity` tests hold the {cpp} side.

| The hint of `peer_connect_failed`
| A connection that fails after its descriptions were exchanged, which the loopback of the lanes never fails; the `room` test fails one over the memory relays.

| A peer kicked from a room, or that kicked us, over a live connection; frames after the higher id's own kick; a connection whose last room was kicked
| A kick follows a room proof that fails over a live connection, and two {cpp} apps never send one: an app has one password for every room, and apps of different passwords share no connection. A Trystero JS 0.26.0 page that mixed passwords did, so the interop lane's wrong room proofs showed trystero's side, against a page and not a twin (<<trystero-testing-lanes>>), and the `shared` test holds the rest over the memory relays.

| Reuse over a live connection
| Only a forged announcement shows it: a third party that publishes, in a peer's name, a room token that peer never advertised, which neither API publishes. The `shared` test forges one over the memory relays.

| A room frame before the peer's presence for the room
| Both APIs advertise a room before its first frame on a connection, so only a presence lost on the way shows it: Chrome drops it, rarely, when a page sends as its answering channel opens, and the interop lane's delayed-open scenario met it; the `connection` and `shared` tests drop it over the memory relays.

| Announcements after a peer leaves
| What differs is what a room publishes to its relays after a peer leaves it while their connection stays, which neither API reports: the peers see the same joins and leaves.

| Presence after binding
| A frame on the connection, which neither API reports; what it changes is how soon the other side binds, a time that no two runs print alike.

| An answer to an earlier challenge
| It needs a handshake answer that arrives after a newer challenge, a race between two bindings that no program sets up on a real network; the `shared` test holds it over the memory relays, whose frames it holds and releases.

| Relay sockets and the Nostr key: the key
| Which key signs a room's events only the relay sees; neither a room nor a peer reports it. The sockets are shown (<<trystero-differences-relay-sockets>>).

| `idle_timeout`
| Not a behaviour: both keep an idle connection 123,333 ms, and Trystero JS 0.26.0 took the same setting under its test-only name, so a program and its twin would agree.

| Frames waiting for a bound room's handler
| A state Trystero JS 0.26.0 passed through between a binding and its room's data handler, which its rooms set as they bound; no program reaches it through the API.

| Renegotiation and media over a shared connection
| Not ported: trystero has nothing of them to run.

| Duplicate connections
| Only two connections whose channels open within a millisecond of each other, from two rooms meeting the peer at once, make a pair that is crossed, which no program sets up on demand: the interop lane's two-room scenarios met one now and then, and the `app_impl` and `shared` tests hold each outcome over the memory relays.

| A Nostr event a relay repeats, or that several relays carry
| What an event changes when it comes twice is the signaling's work, offers and answers made again, which neither API reports: a program sees one join either way, and over honest relays an event's id is always its own. The `protocol` and `room` tests hold the dedup, forged ids included.

| A relay's text, a signal or a handshake message past its bound; a protocol text of thousands of values, or nested deeper than 16
| No Trystero peer writes such a text, so only a relay or a peer that means harm sends one, and what changes is whether the room reads it, which neither API reports. The `protocol` and `signaling` tests hold the bounds.
