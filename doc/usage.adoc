[#trystero-usage]
== Rooms and actions

A room is what Trystero's `joinRoom` returns, and an action what its
`makeAction` returns: this section is the {cpp} form of both.

[#trystero-usage-joining]
=== Joining

An `app` is this program in one application: one peer id, and one connection
to each remote peer, which every room of the app it shares with that peer
uses, as a Trystero JS page shares them. Constructing it takes the executor
every handler and completion runs on, the backend, which must outlive it,
and its configuration, `app_config`, moved. A room is joined with `join`,
which returns a `boost::system::result<room>` to test, or an error:
`error::already_joined` while a room of that id is joined in the app, and
`error::too_many_rooms` when 64 rooms count, since a peer drops a connection
that advertises more. Joining an id in its 99 ms farewell finishes that
farewell first and joins afresh.

`join` and the app's other methods run where its executor runs. An app and
all its rooms use that executor; a multithreaded `io_context` needs a strand.
Destroying or moving over the app handle releases it; its rooms keep the
app's state alive and continue. Two `app` objects, even with the same app
id, are two peers. No registry or identity is kept in static storage.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| `app_config` member | What it is

| `app_id`
| The application's id, Trystero's `appId`: rooms of different apps never meet.

| `self_id`
| One id for every room of the app; empty, 20 random characters drawn once.
  An id longer than 64 bytes is replaced and reported. This id links the
  app's rooms: rooms that must not be linked to one program use an `app`
  each (<<trystero-protocol-limitations>>).

| `relay_urls`
| The Nostr relays. Empty, `relay_redundancy` of Trystero's 29 defaults,
  shuffled with the app id so every peer picks the same ones. Their
  configuration is the app's; sockets and the Nostr signing key remain per room.

| `relay_redundancy`
| The number of default relays to use, 5.

| `ice_servers`
| The STUN and TURN servers; empty, `default_ice_servers()`. An unusable
  URL, or a TURN server without a user name and credential, is dropped and
  reported to `app::on_error` with `error::invalid_configuration`.

| `idle_timeout`
| How long a connection with no rooms stays after a departure, 123,333 ms;
  0 closes it immediately. A negative value becomes 0 and is reported. A
  connection whose last binding was kicked closes at once.

| `log`
| One sink for the app and its rooms, called with a category (`app`, `nostr`,
  `signal`, `webrtc`, `room` or `action`) and a sentence composed in the
  app's resource. Empty, no text is composed.

| `memory`
| The app's resource, `std::pmr::get_default_resource()` by default; it
  must be non-null (<<trystero-allocation>>).
|===

`app_config` is move-only because its log sink is a `handler`: pass it
moved, or build it in place. Each room adds `room_options`:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| `room_options` member | What it is

| `password`
| Part of the key the signals are sealed with and the room's challenge
  handshake. Empty, signals are sealed with the app and room ids alone,
  and the handshake has no challenge. A passwordless room is also open to
  peers connected through another room of the app (<<trystero-protocol-limitations>>).

| `handshake_timeout`
| How long a binding has to finish the handshake, 10 s.

| `max_receive_bytes`
| The most bytes the room reserves for incoming transfers across peers, 256 MiB.

| `memory`
| The room's own resource; null uses the app's resource.
|===

[#trystero-usage-migration]
==== Moving from `room_config`

`room(executor, backend, room_config, room_id)` and `room_config` are gone:
a room is joined through an `app`.

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Previous use | New use

| Standalone room construction
| Construct `app` with the executor, backend and `app_config`, then call
  `join` with the room id and `room_options`; test the returned result.

| `room_config::{app_id, self_id, relay_urls, relay_redundancy, ice_servers}`
| `app_config`

| `room_config::log`
| `app_config::log`: one sink for the app and its rooms, with sentences in the app's resource

| `room_config::{password, handshake_timeout, max_receive_bytes}`
| `room_options`

| `room_config::memory`
| `app_config::memory`, with `room_options::memory` for a room's own resource

| Configuration reports from `room::on_error`, with an empty peer id
| `app::on_error`; `room::on_error` reports problems with peers

| Two rooms with separate peer ids to meet Trystero JS in both
| Two calls to `join` on one `app`

| Two peers in one process
| Two `app` objects

| Rooms that must not be linked to one program
| One `app` each
|===

[#trystero-usage-peers]
=== Peers

`on_peer_join` and `on_peer_leave` set what is called with each peer whose
handshake completed, and with each joined peer that leaves; `peers()` lists
the joined peers. `on_error` sets what is called with each problem with one
peer that the room handled itself, as Trystero's `onPeerError`: a peer with
another password, a handshake that timed out, a connection the backend
refused. It is called with an empty peer id for a count of signals
that did not open with the room key (<<trystero-protocol-bounds>>). Each
handler replaces the one set before, and every view it receives is valid for
the call.

`async_ping` measures the round trip to a joined peer, from sending
`@_ping` to receiving its `@_pong`.

`async_leave` says goodbye to every peer, Trystero's `@_leave`, gives the
farewell 99 ms, as Trystero waits, then detaches its bindings and closes its relay
sockets, and every pending operation completes with `error::room_left`.
Destroying a room leaves at once, without the 99 ms, and so does assigning
another room over a joined one: every peer is told, and every pending
operation completes with `error::room_left`, even while an `action` of it
lives.

A kick or a peer's `@_leave` ends only that room's binding: the app's other
rooms keep using the connection. When it stays live, rebinding waits one
step after the end, initially the larger of `handshake_timeout` and 10 s,
plus 1 s, doubling after each further end to at most 5 minutes. A completed
handshake or the connection's end resets the count. After its own kick the
lower id refuses bindings until that time and keeps frames; the higher id
drops frames until it sends presence, but accepts the lower id's presence.
A binding also requires the peer to have advertised the token itself.
Only the lower id reports a wrong password over a shared connection: the
higher id answers the challenge and sees the peer leave without a report.

[#trystero-usage-actions]
=== Actions

An action is a named channel of messages, wire compatible with Trystero's
`makeAction`. Its name has 1 to 32 UTF-8 bytes and no NUL; with another,
every send fails with `error::invalid_action_name`. Its type says how
messages travel and how they are received:

[cols="2,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Type | Trystero format | Received as

| `std::string` | text | a copy of the bytes
| `std::pmr::string` | text | a copy of the bytes, in the room's resource
| `std::string_view` | text | a view of the frame it arrived in, valid during the handler
| `boost::json::value` | JSON | parsed, in the room's JSON storage
| `trystero::bytes` | binary | a copy of the bytes
| `trystero::pmr_bytes` | binary | the transfer's buffer, moved when resources match, otherwise copied once into the room's resource
| `std::span<const std::uint8_t>` | binary | a view of the frame, valid during the handler
| `trystero::payload` | any | a `std::variant` of `std::string`, `boost::json::value` and `bytes`, by format
|===

A message of another format than the action's type is not delivered to it.
Several actions of different types may share a name: one receives as views,
another sends by moving strings, as the chat does
(<<trystero-browser-chat>>).

Messages arrive at the handler `on_message` sets, with a `message_info`: the
sender's id and the metadata it passed. Without a handler they queue, up to
1024 per action, the oldest dropped first, for `async_receive`, which needs
an owning type: a view would outlive the message. Frames of an action that
arrived before it was made are kept, 64 per peer for 120 s, and delivered
to it once it is.

`async_send` sends to `send_options::targets`, or to every joined peer, with
optional JSON metadata. A payload that fits one 16 KiB frame with its header
and metadata travels inline; a larger one is offered, accepted by the
receiver within 120 s, and sent in chunks of at most 16,370 bytes. A frame
or a chunk is handed to the data channel only while the channel buffers at
most 64 KiB, waiting at most 10 s for it to drain. `async_send` completes
once each target's local data channel has taken the inline frame, or the
last chunk of the transfer the receiver accepted, with the first failure if
any, as Trystero's `send` resolves: nothing confirms that the receiver has
the message. A receiver takes one transfer per peer at a time, accepts it only for
an action with a receiver, and reserves at most `max_receive_bytes` across
peers.

[#trystero-usage-tokens]
=== Completion tokens and ownership

Every asynchronous operation is an Asio initiating function. With a callback
it calls it on the room's executor; with `boost::asio::use_awaitable` it
resumes a coroutine; with `boost::asio::deferred` it returns an operation
started later.

Because a deferred operation starts after the call, the arguments of an
asynchronous operation are owned: `async_send` takes its value by value,
moved when the type owns its storage, copied once at the call into the room's
resource when it is a view, and serialized at once when it is a
`boost::json::value`; `send_options` owns its strings; and `async_ping`
copies the peer id at the call. Everything else that only reads text takes a
`std::string_view`.

[#trystero-usage-errors]
=== Errors

Operations complete with a `boost::system::error_code` in the category
`webcpp.trystero`, of the enumeration `error`:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Value | Meaning

| `room_left` | the room was left while the operation was pending
| `no_such_peer` | the target is not a joined peer
| `peer_disconnected` | the peer went away before the operation finished
| `invalid_action_name` | an action's name is not 1 to 32 UTF-8 bytes without NUL
| `metadata_too_large` | the metadata does not fit one frame
| `transfer_rejected` | the receiver refused the transfer, or a message had another format than the awaited type
| `transfer_timed_out` | the receiver neither accepted nor refused within 120 s
| `channel_stalled` | the data channel stopped draining for 10 s
| `wrong_password` | a peer's signals or handshake used another password, or a handshake message had the wrong shape
| `handshake_timed_out` | a connected peer did not finish the handshake in time
| `handshake_failed` | reserved: nothing reports it today
| `backend_failure` | the browser or the native stack refused an operation
| `invalid_configuration` | the app dropped an unusable configuration value and continues without it
| `already_joined` | a room of that id is already joined in the app
| `too_many_rooms` | the app already holds 64 rooms, including farewells
|===
