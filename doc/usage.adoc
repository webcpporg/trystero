[#trystero-usage]
== Rooms and actions

A room is what Trystero's `joinRoom` returns, and an action what its
`makeAction` returns: this section is the {cpp} form of both, as 0.27.0
has them.

[#trystero-usage-joining]
=== Joining

An `app` is this program in one application and password domain: one
endpoint identity, a P-256 key it makes when it is made, whose public key
derives its peer id; one user identity, the one its configuration gives or
an ephemeral one it makes; one immutable password; its relays and its ICE
servers. Each room of the app negotiates its own connection to each remote
peer, as each room of 0.27.0 does: the app shares its identity, its relays'
configuration and its rooms' keys with its rooms, and no connection.
Constructing it takes the executor every listener and completion runs on,
the backend, which must outlive it, and its configuration, `app_config`,
moved. A room is joined with `join`, which returns a
`boost::system::result<room>` to test, or an error:
`error::invalid_max_users` for a `room_options::max_users` of 0 or above
2^53^ - 1, as 0.27.0's `joinRoom` throws, and `error::already_joined` while
a room of that id is joined in the app, as 0.27.0 refuses a second join.
Joining an id in its 99 ms farewell finishes that farewell first and joins
afresh. A room of an app with a password announces itself once the app has
derived its keys, off the room's thread; `join` returns before
(<<trystero-design-port>>).

`join` and the app's other methods run where its executor runs. An app and
all its rooms use that executor; a multithreaded `io_context` needs a strand.
Destroying or moving over the app handle releases it; its rooms keep the
app's state alive and continue. Two `app` objects, even with the same app
id, are two peers, of one user when they are given one `user_identity`.
Rooms with different passwords use separate apps with distinct peer ids. A
room has no password override; changing the domain password requires a new
app. No registry or identity is kept in static storage.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| `app_config` member | What it is

| `app_id`
| The application's id, Trystero's `appId`: rooms of different apps never meet.

| `password`
| The immutable password shared by every room of this app, copied into the
  app's resource. Each room derives its keys from it
  (<<trystero-protocol-crypto>>), which seal its signals: a peer of another
  password opens none of them, and never connects, as in 0.27.0, which has
  no password challenge. Empty, the app is passwordless, its signals still
  sealed with the app and room ids. Different passwords require separate
  apps and distinct peer ids.

| `relay_urls`
| The Nostr relays. Empty, `relay_redundancy` of 0.27.0's 30 defaults, in
  the order its seeded shuffle gives them for the app id, so every peer
  picks the same ones. Their configuration is the app's; sockets and the
  Nostr signing key remain per room.

| `relay_redundancy`
| The number of default relays to use, 5.

| `ice_servers`
| The STUN and TURN servers; empty, `default_ice_servers()`. An unusable
  URL, or a TURN server without a user name and credential, is dropped and
  reported to `app::on_error` with `error::invalid_configuration`.

| `identity`
| The user the app's rooms present, a `user_identity` the program made or
  imported (0.27.0's `'custom'`); absent, the app makes an ephemeral one
  (0.27.0's `'none'`), lost with the app (<<trystero-usage-peers>>).

| `log`
| One sink for the app and its rooms, called with a category (`app`, `nostr`,
  `signal`, `webrtc`, `room` or `action`) and a sentence composed in the
  app's resource. Empty, no text is composed.

| `memory`
| The app's resource, `std::pmr::get_default_resource()` by default; it
  must be non-null (<<trystero-allocation>>).
|===

It has no `self_id`: 0.27.0 has no custom peer id, and an app derives its
id, 20 base62 characters, from its endpoint key, which `app::self_id()` and
`room::self()` give. `app_config` is move-only because its log sink is a
`handler`: pass it moved, or build it in place. Each room adds
`room_options`:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| `room_options` member | What it is

| `max_receive_bytes`
| The most bytes the room reserves for incoming transfers across peers, 256 MiB.

| `max_json_document_bytes`
| The largest image a received JSON payload's document may take of the room's resource, webcpp::document's default, 64 MiB; a payload past it is not delivered (<<trystero-protocol-json>>).

| `max_users`
| The most users the room holds, its own included, Trystero's `maxUsers`; empty, no bound. A peer of a user already in the room is admitted when it is full; a peer of a new user is refused once its handshake passed, and its room reports `room_full` (<<trystero-usage-peers>>).

| `memory`
| The room's own resource; null uses the app's resource.
|===

It has no `handshake_timeout`: 0.27.0 removed `handshakeTimeoutMs`, and a
peer has 10 s to finish the handshake.

[#trystero-usage-migration]
==== Moving from trystero 0.1.0

trystero 0.1.0 ported Trystero 0.26.0. Its programs move as Trystero's own
programs move from 0.26.0 to 0.27.0, and its rooms meet only Trystero
0.27.0's now, as 0.27.0's meet no 0.26.0 room:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| In 0.1.0 | Now

| `app_config::self_id`
| Gone: the app derives its id from the endpoint key it makes;
  `app::self_id()` and `room::self()` give it.

| `room_options::handshake_timeout`
| Gone: a peer has 10 s.

| `app_config::idle_timeout`, `app::max_rooms` and `error::too_many_rooms`
| Gone with the connection an app's rooms shared: each room has its own
  connections, closed as it leaves, and an app joins any number of rooms.

| `error::wrong_password` and `error::handshake_timed_out`
| Gone with 0.26.0's password handshake: a peer of another password is
  reported as `decrypt_failed`, and a handshake that fails, its 10 s
  included, as `handshake_failed` with 0.27.0's reason. The values of
  `error` that followed them moved: a program compares codes with the
  enumerators, never with numbers it kept.

| `on_peer_join(f)`, `on_peer_leave(f)`, `on_error(f)` and an action's
  `on_message(f)`, each setting the one handler and replacing the one before
| Each adds a listener beside those added before and returns its
  `subscription`, whose `unsubscribe()` removes it; `listen_options` has
  `once` and, for joins, `replay` (<<trystero-usage-listeners>>).

| A join listener called with a peer's id
| Called with a `peer_event`: the peer's id and its user's.

| `on_error(f)` called with a peer, a code and a text
| Called with a `room_error`: 0.27.0's code, its message, the app's and the
  room's ids, and the context of its code (<<trystero-usage-errors>>).

| `peers()`, the joined peers' ids
| A map of each active peer's id to its `peer_info`, its user's id among
  it; `users()` lists the users, and `self()` this peer.

| A send to a target that has not joined, `error::no_such_peer`
| `error::not_connected`; a target may be a user's id, which stands for its
  active peers.

| Frames of an action that arrived before it was made, kept 64 per peer for
  120 s
| Dropped, and their transfers rejected, as 0.27.0 keeps none: a room makes
  the actions it receives right after it is joined.

| No user: a peer was its id
| A peer's user is the one its handshake verified; an app presents
  `app_config::identity`, or an ephemeral user, and a program keeps one
  across runs with `export_user_identity` and `import_user_identity`.

| A room of any number of peers
| `room_options::max_users` bounds its users, and `join` refuses a bound of
  0 with `error::invalid_max_users`.

| `backend::websocket::send` returns nothing
| It returns whether the socket queued the text, for a backend of a
  program's own (<<trystero-differences-behaviour>>).
|===

`room(executor, backend, room_config, room_id)` and `room_config`, of
trystero's earlier versions, are gone too: a room is joined through an
`app`, the members of `room_config` are those of `app_config` and
`room_options`, and two peers in one process are two `app` objects.

[#trystero-usage-peers]
=== Peers and users

A peer is one endpoint in the room, a user who it is: in 0.27.0 several
peers, the tabs of one person, may be one user. An app's peer id derives
from the P-256 endpoint key it makes when it is made, and its user id from
its user identity, `app_config::identity` or an ephemeral one the app makes
(`app::self_id`, `app::user_id`). A `user_identity` is made with
`create_user_identity`, kept as the text `export_user_identity` writes and
read again with `import_user_identity`, 0.27.0's `trystero_uk_` text or the
JSON of a private P-256 key; several apps given one are one user's
endpoints. A remote peer joins once 0.27.0's identity handshake has
verified its endpoint key and its user key, both bound to the DTLS
fingerprints of the connection, and its user id is the one it proved.

`on_peer_join` adds a listener of each peer whose handshake completed, with
its `peer_event`, and `on_peer_leave` one of each joined peer that leaves;
`on_user_join` adds a listener of each user whose first peer joined, after
that peer's `on_peer_join`, and `on_user_leave` one of each user whose last
peer left, after that peer's `on_peer_leave`. `peers()` maps each active
peer's id to its `peer_info`: its user's id, whether it is reached
directly, always until relayed peers are ported, its distance, 1, and its
roles, none until roles are ported. `users()` maps each user's id to its
active peers, in the order they became active, and its roles; this peer's
own user is left out. `self()` is this peer at once, synchronously, since
an app's identity exists from its construction on: its peer and user ids,
the identity's persistence, `ephemeral` for one the app made, `imported` for
`app_config::identity`'s, the room's id, the id it was
joined as, and its roles. The maps are a `std::pmr::map` over the room's
resource with a transparent comparator, so a program looks a peer up by a
`std::string_view`.

`on_error` adds a listener of each problem the room handled itself, as
Trystero's `onError`: each is a `room_error` (<<trystero-usage-errors>>).
Every view a listener receives is valid for the call.

`async_ping` measures the round trip to an active peer, from sending the
request `@_ping` to receiving its answer, or completes with
`error::no_such_peer`.  It has no timeout, as Trystero's, and completes with
`error::room_left` or `error::peer_disconnected` when the room or the peer goes.

`async_leave` says goodbye to every peer, Trystero's `@_leave`, gives the
farewell 99 ms, as Trystero waits, then closes its connections and its relay
sockets, and every pending operation completes with `error::room_left`.
Destroying a room leaves at once, without the 99 ms, and so does assigning
another room over a joined one: every peer is told, and every pending
operation completes with `error::room_left`, even while an `action` of it
lives.

A room admits a peer as 0.27.0's handshake manager does. Once the data
channel opens, the side whose id is lower in UTF-16 order initiates: it
sends its nonce, the DTLS fingerprints of its description, its user id and
its endpoint and user keys; the other answers with its own and its two
signatures of the transcript, which binds the app, the room, both ids, both
nonces, both sides' fingerprints and both users; the initiator verifies
them, signs the transcript too, and the responder verifies those
(<<trystero-protocol-handshake>>). Each side then sends its ready frame,
and the peer joins once both did. Until then the peer is pending: absent
from `peers()` and `users()`, and its frames other than the handshake's and
`@_leave` are not delivered. A handshake that does not complete in 10 s, a
step that does not verify, fingerprints that are not the connection's, or
more than 16 messages the handshake has not read, end the peer: its room
reports `handshake_failed` with 0.27.0's reason, once a handshake, and the
peer is told to leave.

In a room with `room_options::max_users`, a peer whose handshake passed is
admitted when its user is in the room already, or the users, those of
admissions under way included, are fewer than the bound; else it is refused
with a ready frame saying "room full", and its room reports `room_full`
with the refusing peer's id. The room counts users, not peers: another tab
of a user present is welcome in a full room. It does not dial a peer it
refused again while it stays full.

[#trystero-usage-listeners]
=== Listeners and subscriptions

Every `on_*` of a room, of an action and of an app adds a listener and
returns a `subscription`, as each of Trystero's returns the function that
removes it: `unsubscribe()` removes the listener, and destroying the token
leaves it, as dropping Trystero's function does. Listeners of one event run
in the order added, each event reported from the room's executor; one added
while an event is reported waits for the next, and one removed before its
turn does not run. `listen_options::once` removes a listener before its
first run, and `replay`, which only the joins of peers and users take, calls
it first, within the call that adds it, with each peer or user already
joined. Trystero's `signal`, an `AbortSignal`, has no {cpp} counterpart:
`unsubscribe` replaces it. A subscription requires the resource of what
keeps its listener to outlive it.

An action's `on_receive` and a request action's `on_request` take one
listener at a time, and return a `boost::system::result<subscription>`:
`error::already_subscribed` while one is in place, as Trystero throws.

[#trystero-usage-actions]
=== Actions

An action is a named channel of messages, wire compatible with Trystero's
`makeAction`. Its name has 1 to 32 UTF-8 bytes and no NUL; with another,
every send fails with `error::invalid_action_name`. Its type says how
messages travel and how they are received:

[cols="2,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Type | Trystero format | Received as

| `std::string` | text | a copy of the bytes
| `std::pmr::string` | text | a copy of the bytes, in the room's resource
| `std::string_view` | text | a view of the frame it arrived in, valid during the listener
| `webcpp::document::document` | JSON | parsed into a document of its own, with the standard allocator
| `webcpp::document::pmr::document` | JSON | parsed into a document of the room's resource, which holds its image and the parse's working state
| `trystero::bytes` | binary | a copy of the bytes
| `trystero::pmr_bytes` | binary | the payload's buffer in the room's resource, moved
| `std::span<const std::uint8_t>` | binary | a view of the frame, valid during the listener
| `trystero::payload` | any | a `std::variant` of `std::string`, `webcpp::document::document` and `bytes`, by format
|===

A JSON payload is webcpp::document's: a `webcpp::document::document` the
program builds or parses, with the standard allocator, or a
`webcpp::document::pmr::document`, whose image a memory resource holds, the
room's when it is received; the pair mirrors `std::string` and
`std::pmr::string`. A {cpp} peer sends the
bytes `JSON.stringify` writes of the same value, which Trystero JS sends,
for every value JavaScript can hold: its strings, booleans and nulls,
its keys in JavaScript's order, array indices first, and every number a
double holds, written as JavaScript writes it. A number a double cannot
hold, an integer past 2^53^ or a decimal of more digits, is sent exact, as
webcpp::document holds it: a Trystero JS peer reads the nearest double, or
`Infinity` past a double's range. A text that does not parse is not
delivered, nor one nested deeper than 256 or holding a lone surrogate,
which `JSON.parse` takes, nor one whose document passes the room's
`room_options::max_json_document_bytes`, 64 MiB of image by default, some
350,000 values of one character, which a program lowers to bound what a
peer's JSON may take of its room's resource (<<trystero-protocol-json>>).

A document is moved, never copied: so `payload`, which may hold one, has no
copy assignment, and in a build without exceptions no copy constructor
either; nor has a document a default constructor there, where a program
makes one with `create()` or `webcpp::document::parse`, each of which
returns a result, given the resource's allocator for a
`webcpp::document::pmr::document`. A handle of a document's values refers to
the document object, as every handle of webcpp::document does: moving the
document invalidates it, so a handler that keeps a received document moves
it and reads it through the handles of the document it moved it into. A
`webcpp::document::pmr::document` move-assigned to one of another resource
copies its image into that resource, and raises `std::bad_alloc` through
`boost::throw_exception` when the resource has no room: a program without
exceptions keeps one by move construction, `std::optional::emplace` for
one. The room test sends one:

[source]
----
include::{library-root}/test/room_test.cpp[tag=json-send,indent=0]
----

receives it:

[source]
----
include::{library-root}/test/room_test.cpp[tag=json-receive,indent=0]
----

and reads it:

[source]
----
include::{library-root}/test/room_test.cpp[tag=json-read,indent=0]
----

A message of another format than the action's type is not delivered to it.
Several actions of different types may share a name: one receives as views,
another sends by moving strings, as the chat does
(<<trystero-platform-chat>>).

Messages arrive at the listeners `on_message` adds, each with a
`message_info`: the sender's id and the metadata it passed. Each listener
decodes the message, so with several each takes its own copy. Until the
action listens, its messages wait, as 0.27.0's wait for a listener, 32 per
sender for 10 s, the oldest dropped first. An action listens once it has a
listener, and from its first `async_receive` on, which needs an owning type,
since a view would outlive the message: once awaited, the messages no
receive waits for queue for the next, up to 1024 per action, the oldest
dropped first. A message of another format, or a JSON text that does not
parse, completes it with `error::transfer_rejected` and an empty value,
which allocates nothing: an empty container, in the room's resource for a
`std::pmr` type, and for a JSON one webcpp::document's `nothing()`, a
document with no image whose root reads `undefined`. Frames of an action
the room has not made are dropped, and its transfers rejected, as 0.27.0
does: a room makes the actions it receives right after it is joined.

`on_receive` sets the action's receive gate, Trystero's `onReceive`, asked
about each message as it arrives, with its sender, its size and its
metadata, before the action receives it: a refused inline message is
dropped, and a refused transfer rejected with "payload rejected by
receiver", which completes its sender's send with
`error::transfer_rejected`. It runs during the frame's processing and
answers at once; Trystero's may also answer with a promise. Without a gate,
a room accepts every transfer within `max_receive_bytes`, as Trystero does.
`on_receive_progress` adds a listener of the share of each transfer received
after each chunk but the last, then 1 once the message is whole.

`async_send` sends to `send_options::targets`, each an active peer's id or
a user's, which stands for each of its active peers (a peer reached twice
is sent to once), or to every active peer, with optional JSON metadata; to
a room without peers it succeeds, and to a target that is neither an active
peer nor a user of one it completes with `error::not_connected`. A payload
that fits one 16 KiB frame with its header and metadata travels inline; a
larger one is offered, accepted by the receiver within 120 s, and sent in
chunks of at most 16,370 bytes (<<trystero-protocol-channel>>). A frame or a
chunk is handed to the data channel only while the channel buffers at most
64 KiB, waiting at most 10 s for it to drain. `async_send` completes once
each target's local data channel has taken the inline frame, or the last
chunk of the transfer the receiver accepted, with the first failure if any,
as Trystero's `send` resolves: nothing confirms that the receiver has the
message. A receiver reserves at most `max_receive_bytes` across peers.

[#trystero-usage-requests]
==== Request actions

`make_request_action<T, R>` makes a request action, Trystero's `makeAction`
with `kind: 'request'`: `async_request` asks one peer, or the active peer of
a user with the lowest id, as 0.27.0 picks one, with
`request_options::metadata` and an optional `timeout`, and completes with
`(error_code, R)`, the answer. The peer asked answers with the listener
`on_request` sets, which returns an `R`, or a `boost::system::result<R>`
whose error refuses the request: the requester's request then completes
with `error::request_rejected`, without the reason, which the room logs;
`error::request_timed_out` after the timeout, `error::not_connected` for a
target not connected, `error::peer_disconnected` when the peer leaves first.
An answer that answers no request is dropped, as 0.27.0 drops it: its offer
is rejected "unexpected response", and nothing is reported; the peer stays.
The listener answers at once; Trystero's may answer with a promise. A name
is a message action or a request action, not both: a handle of the other
kind completes its operations with `error::action_redefined`, where
Trystero throws. `requestMany`, and the `onProgress` and `signal` of sends
and requests, are not ported (<<trystero-differences>>).

[#trystero-usage-connectivity]
=== Relay connectivity

`app::set_relay_connectivity` sets how the relays of every room of the app
connect, Trystero's `setRelayConnectivity`, for this app alone, since a
process may hold several: `offline` closes every relay socket of the app's
rooms and holds them closed, those of a room joined meanwhile too; `online`
reconnects at once every relay not connected, which then subscribes and
announces again; `automatic`, the default, follows the page's `online` and
`offline` events, and natively, where there are none, is online. A
connection already made goes on while the relays are offline.

[#trystero-usage-tokens]
=== Completion tokens and ownership

Every asynchronous operation is an Asio initiating function. With a callback
it calls it on the room's executor; with `webcpp::asio::use_awaitable` it
resumes a coroutine; with `webcpp::asio::deferred` it returns an operation
started later. webcpp::asio is Boost.Asio itself natively, so Boost.Asio's
tokens are the same there; on emscripten they are webcpp::asio's.

Because a deferred operation starts after the call, the arguments of an
asynchronous operation are owned: `async_send` takes its value by value,
moved when the type owns its storage, copied once at the call into the room's
resource when it is a view, and written at once, into the room's resource,
when it is a JSON document; `send_options` owns its strings; and
`async_ping` copies the peer id at the call. Everything else that only reads text takes a
`std::string_view`.

[#trystero-usage-errors]
=== Errors

Operations complete with a `boost::system::error_code` in the category
`webcpp.trystero`, of the enumeration `error`, whose messages are 0.27.0's
where it has one. `room::on_error` reports a problem the room handled itself
as a `room_error`: its code, its message, the app's and the room's ids, and,
for the codes that have them, the peer's id, the stage of a
`decrypt_failed` (offer or answer), whether a TURN server was configured
for a `peer_connect_failed`, the reason of a `banned` and the plugin of a
`plugin_failed`. `room_error_code_name` gives 0.27.0's name of a room
error's code, `decrypt-failed` and the others. `app::on_error` reports what
the app set aside of its configuration, as `error::invalid_configuration`
with no room nor peer.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Value | Meaning

| `room_left` | the room was left while the operation was pending
| `no_such_peer` | the peer pinged is not an active peer
| `peer_disconnected` | the peer went away before the operation finished
| `invalid_action_name` | an action's name is not 1 to 32 UTF-8 bytes without NUL
| `metadata_too_large` | the metadata does not fit one frame
| `transfer_rejected` | the receiver refused the transfer, or a message had another format than the awaited type
| `transfer_timed_out` | the receiver neither accepted nor refused within 120 s
| `channel_stalled` | the data channel stopped draining for 10 s
| `handshake_failed` | 0.27.0's `handshake-failed`, reported by the room whose handshake with a connected peer failed, once a handshake, with 0.27.0's reason: a step that does not verify, fingerprints that are not the connection's, more than 16 unread messages, or 10 s passed ("handshake timed out after 10000ms"); the peer is told to leave
| `backend_failure` | the browser or the native stack refused an operation, or a room's keys could not be derived
| `invalid_configuration` | the app dropped an unusable configuration value and continues without it
| `already_joined` | a room of that id is already joined in the app
| `room_full` | 0.27.0's `room-full`, reported by the room a peer's full room refused, with that peer's id, once its handshake verified the peer; the peer goes, and is not announced to again
| `plugin_failed` | 0.27.0's `plugin-failed`, which nothing reports: plugins are not ported
| `banned` | 0.27.0's `banned`, reported by the room a pending peer refused with its ready frame, with that peer's id and its reason, when it gave one, in the message; a peer has no roles to ban with, so only a Trystero JS peer sends one
| `peer_error` | a peer's frame was malformed or against the protocol; its message says which, as 0.27.0's, and the peer is told to leave and goes
| `identity_failed` | the app's endpoint key or its ephemeral user identity could not be made (a system without a generator): each room it joins reports it once the join has returned, then ends without reaching a relay
| `relay_failed` | the first connection of every relay closed before any opened, or no relay took its first announcement and the others failed to open; reported once, with the failure's text when there is one
| `decrypt_failed` | an offer or an answer did not open with the room key: another password, a corrupted or forged signal, or a plaintext that is not UTF-8; reported at most once a minute per peer and stage
| `peer_connect_failed` | the connection with a peer did not open after the exchange of descriptions; its message hints at TURN when none is configured
| `already_subscribed` | a listener that takes one at a time is already set
| `not_connected` | a target of a send or a request is neither an active peer nor a user of one
| `action_aborted` | 0.27.0's "operation aborted"; nothing aborts an action before its `signal` is ported
| `request_rejected` | the peer asked refused the request
| `request_timed_out` | the request was not answered within its timeout
| `action_redefined` | the action's name was made the other kind of action first
| `invalid_user_identity` | `import_user_identity` was given a text that is no user key, a point that is not on the curve, or a scalar that is not its point's
| `invalid_max_users` | `app::join` was given a `room_options::max_users` of 0 or above 2^53^ - 1
|===
