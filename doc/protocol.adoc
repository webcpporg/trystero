[#trystero-protocol]
== The protocol

A room keeps Trystero 0.26.0's wire byte for byte. The protocol's pure
functions are in `<webcpp/trystero/protocol/>`; their tests compare them,
byte for byte, with values Trystero JS computed itself
(<<trystero-testing>>). What a Trystero JS peer or a page sees never changes:
the topic prefixes `Trystero@` and `Trystero:`, the handshake's
`+__trystero_pw+`, every constant and message layout, and the default relays
in their order.

[#trystero-protocol-discovery]
=== Discovery over Nostr

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Part | What it is

| Root topic
| Trystero's `sha1` of `Trystero@<app>@<room>`: each SHA-1 byte in base 36,
  unpadded, concatenated

| Peer topic
| the same of `Trystero@<app>@<room>@<peer id>`, where a peer receives its
  offers, answers and candidates

| Event
| kind 20000 plus the sum of the topic's UTF-16 code units modulo 10000,
  ephemeral; the topic in an `x` tag; signed with BIP-340

| Subscription
| one per relay, to the root topic and the peer's own topic

| Announcement
| `+{"peerId":...,"nonce":...}+` on the root topic: at once, then after 233,
  533, 1333 and 5333 ms, then every 60 s

| Relays
| the configured ones, or 5 of Trystero's 29 defaults, shuffled with
  `sin(seed{plus}{plus}) * 10000` seeded by the app id
|===

A relay that answers `rate-limited:` is skipped for 60 s, doubling to 15
minutes while the replies persist; one that answers `blocked:`,
`restricted:`, `auth-required:` or `pow:` is retired. A lost relay is
reconnected after a random share of 3.3 s, doubling to 60 s, and the delay
comes back to 3.3 s only once a connection stayed open for 60 s, so that a
relay that closes the room at once after each open is tried less and less
often.

[#trystero-protocol-signaling]
=== Signaling

Of two peers, the one with the lower id offers, on the relay that carried the
other's announcement. The higher one answers an announcement by announcing
itself on the other's peer topic, so the lower one offers: at most once a
second per peer, and once in 5.3 s per relay for the whole room, Trystero's
announce interval, so that a flood of low ids does not have the relays
rate-limit the room. Offers, answers and candidates are sealed with AES-GCM
under the SHA-256 of `<password>:<app>:<room>`, the password empty when there
is none, with a 16-byte IV, as the text `<iv>$<ciphertext>`: the IV's bytes
in decimal joined by commas, and the ciphertext with its tag in base64.
Candidates follow their offer or answer, trickled. A negotiation ends when
the data channel opens: the app adopts the connection, and signals from a
bound peer are ignored. A live connection is reused for another room only
after the peer advertised that room's token itself on the connection.

[#trystero-protocol-channel]
=== The data channel

Every message is a room frame: `1`, the length of the room token (16 bits,
big-endian), the token, the hexadecimal SHA-256 of `Trystero:<app>:<room>`,
then an action frame. A presence frame, `2`, present or not, the token's
length and the token, announces or withdraws the room.

Presence advertises every room of the app on each connection. It records
which rooms the remote peer joined and binds matching local rooms; a
withdrawal detaches only that room. The connection routes by token:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Frame | What happens

| An envelope that does not decode
| The connection ends for every room.

| `presence(1, T)`, new token
| Record it; a 65th distinct token ends the connection. Bind the matching
  local room when its binding conditions permit.

| `presence(1, T)`, known token
| Bind the matching unbound local room when permitted.

| `presence(0, T)`
| Forget the token and its kept frames, and end its binding as a departure.

| Room frame, token bound
| Deliver it to that room's action wire.

| Room frame after the higher id's own kick, before it sends presence
| Drop it: it belongs to the ended binding.

| Room frame for another room of the app, not bound to this connection
| Keep it, at most 64 frames per connection, all expiring 10 s after the
  first. Bind only when the peer advertised the token and no backoff runs.

| Room frame for any other token
| Drop it silently; keep the connection.
|===

On binding, kept frames arrive before later frames; a kept `@_leave`
discards itself and everything kept before it for that token. There is no
buffer for a bound room awaiting its handler: delivery is installed in the
same call that makes its binding.

Waiting sends take turns by binding on each channel drain, preserving each
binding's waiter order and the 10 s stall bound. Chunked transfers send one
chunk per drain, giving waiting sends from other rooms a turn. Inline sends
at a buffered amount of at most 64 KiB go straight to the channel: they have
no trystero rate bound, so a flood of pings can delay every room's traffic
to that peer, and that peer only.

Action frames are Trystero's action wire, version 2: an inline frame carries
the payload whole; a larger payload is an offer, accepted or refused by the
receiver, then chunks of at most 16,370 bytes, in order. Integers and
float64 values are big-endian, as JavaScript's `DataView` writes them.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Frame | Bytes

| inline (kind 0) | 2, the kind and the format shifted by four, the name (32 bytes, NUL padded), the metadata's length (uint16), the metadata, the payload
| offer (kind 1) | the inline header, the transfer's id (uint32), the payload's size (float64), the metadata
| chunk (kind 2) | 2, 2, the transfer's id (uint32), the offset (float64), the bytes
| accepted (kind 3) | 2, 3, the transfer's id (uint32)
| rejected (kind 4) | 2, 4, the transfer's id (uint32), the reason (UTF-8, at most 200 bytes)
| cancelled (kind 5) | 2, 5, the transfer's id (uint32)
|===

The payload's format is text (0), JSON (1) or binary (2). Internal actions
run the room:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Action | What it carries

| `@_hsdata` | the password handshake: the lower id sends a challenge, and the other answers with the hexadecimal SHA-256 of `<challenge>:<password>:<app>:<room>`
| `@_hsready` | the sender's side of the handshake passed; a peer joins when both sides sent it
| `@_ping`, `@_pong` | round trips
| `@_leave` | the sender leaves the room
|===

The media actions, `@_stream`, `@_track` and `@_signal`, are not ported
(<<trystero-differences>>): a frame of one is ignored.

[#trystero-protocol-bounds]
=== Bounded signaling

Announcements are not sealed, so anyone who knows the app and room ids can
announce any number of peer ids, and anyone who reads the relays can send any
signal in any peer's name. Trystero JS bounds none of this; trystero bounds
what each costs a room.

* Nothing a peer sends changes a negotiation before it opens with the room
  key: an offer, an answer or a candidate that does not open is counted,
  reported once per negotiation, and changes nothing else. A flood of fresh
  ids makes each a negotiation, so the reports are paced: the first of a
  quiet 10 s goes to `on_error` at once, with its peer, and the others of
  those 10 s in one report, with no peer and their number.
* A room holds at most `detail::signaling::max_unconnected_peers` (32)
  negotiations with peers it is not connected to. A negotiation whose offer
  and answer expire without a connection is dropped, an idle one gives its
  place to a new peer, and a new peer beyond the bound is refused and logged
  once.
* A room makes at most `max_connections_per_window` (64) connections in any
  57 s, however soon they end, and a page at most 256
  (<<trystero-browser-bounds>>); the native backend makes at most 256 a
  minute (<<trystero-native-config>>).
* A signal's fields are bounded in `protocol/signal.hpp`: the peer id (64
  bytes; Trystero's are 20), the offer id (64 bytes), a sealed offer or
  answer (64 KiB) and a sealed candidate (16 KiB, above the 8192 bytes of
  plaintext Trystero takes).
* A relay is a third party too: its texts are bounded at 1 MiB, what the room
  holds unsent for it at 4 MiB, and, natively, its silence at 30 s
  (<<trystero-native-relays>>).

[cols="2,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Bound | Scope | Value and consequence

| Joined rooms
| App
| 64, counting farewells until withdrawal; `join` returns `error::too_many_rooms` beyond it.

| Advertised room tokens
| Connection
| 64 distinct tokens; a 65th ends the connection.

| Kept frames for unbound local rooms
| Connection
| 64, at most 64 x 16,515 bytes; expire together 10 s after the first.
  Other tokens retain nothing.

| Room frame and token
| Connection
| 16,515 bytes, token 1 to 128 bytes; an invalid envelope ends the connection.

| No bindings
| Connection
| `idle_timeout` after a departure, 123,333 ms by default, 0 closes immediately;
  closed at once if its last binding was kicked.

| Handshake
| Room and peer
| `handshake_timeout`, 10 s; failure kicks that binding only.

| Earlier challenges
| Room and peer
| Last 8 since the last completed handshake; a correct answer to any passes.

| Pending action frames
| Room and peer
| 64 for 120 s; one incoming transfer per peer, `max_receive_bytes` across
  peers, and 1024 queued messages per action.
|===

After a kick or the peer's `@_leave`, a live connection is rebound one step
after that binding ended. The initial step is the larger of
`handshake_timeout` and 10 s, plus 1 s (11 s by default), doubling at each
further end to 5 minutes. Only a completed handshake or the connection's
end resets it. After its own kicks the lower id refuses bindings until its
backoff ends, ignores signals and keeps frames; the higher id drops frames
until it next sends presence and accepts the lower id's presence at once.
The room sends presence again on reuse or scheduled rebinding, making the
other side bind without a relay response. Announcements restart only when
no live connection remains. A forged announcement never binds a peer that
has not advertised that token itself.

A connection counts against its negotiating room's 64-per-57-s window even
after the app adopts it; presence and reuse make no connections. Fresh ids
that connect and depart keep at most `64 x ceil(idle_timeout / 57 s)` idle
connections per room, 192 by default, within the backend's own bound.
Leaving a room with many peers retains no more connections than the app
already held. Kept frames and tokens cost at most about 1 MiB and 8 KiB
per connection; each of at most 64 bound rooms retains its own peer bounds.
A flood spends its room's queues, transfers and reservations; routing never
holds one room's frame for another room.

With the lower id a wrong password causes at most six kicks and reports in
the first ten minutes, then one per 5 minutes. The higher id can be rebound
by a lower-id peer at that peer's pace, but reports at most one handshake
timeout per `handshake_timeout` and no wrong password.

[#trystero-protocol-replay]
=== Replayed offers and answers

A sealed offer or answer holds neither its offer id nor a peer id
(Trystero's wire), so one recorded from the relays still opens when anyone
resends it in its sender's name, and so does the room's own, sent back to it.
A room remembers, by the SHA-256 of their opened text, the last 1024 offers
and answers it made or acted on
(`detail::signaling::remembered_descriptions`), and refuses one that comes to
it as a peer's: it changes nothing and is logged. It makes or acts on at most
two per connection it makes, so each is remembered for at least an offer's
lifetime (57 s), and in a quiet room for its whole life. So a room refuses a
sealed offer or answer it acted on, resent, and one it made itself, sent back
as a peer's offer or answer.

[#trystero-protocol-limitations]
=== What remains

Sharing preserves Trystero's wire, including its security limits. A password
protects admission, while an app's identity and presence link its rooms.

*A watcher not connected to the app, without the key, can keep two peers apart.* A sealed offer or
answer the room neither made nor acted on still opens and is acted on. Such
texts are not scarce: every peer seals a fresh offer for any id announced
above its own, and announcements need no key. So anyone who reads and
publishes on the relays, without the key, can keep any two peers of a room
apart from their first meeting for as long as it keeps at it, each attempt
costing the other peer a connection of its window. A watcher not connected to the app cannot read or alter
anything the peers exchange, join the room, or touch peers already joined; a
joined peer that drops is a new peer again. The room refuses only what it
made or already acted on. Only a wire that seals the offer id and both peer
ids closes this, which Trystero would have to share (reported upstream).

*A flooder can delay new peers.* Joined peers, memory and connections stay
safe under a flood, but a flooder who keeps sending new ids takes each place,
or each connection of the window, as it frees, so new peers can be kept out
for as long as the flood lasts, each place up to one offer's lifetime (57 s)
at a time. A joined peer whose connection drops comes back as a new peer,
with the same id, and is kept out the same way: the room has no
renegotiation that would keep its place. Closing it needs announcements that
prove the room key, a wire change Trystero would have to share.

*A passwordless room is open to the app's other peers.* Presence gives
any peer connected through any room the token of every room in the app.
Relay events reveal their topics in the clear, and announcements reveal
the app's peer id. The token alone binds an existing connection; a
passwordless room has no further gate beyond `@_hsready`. Such a peer can
echo a token and join without knowing that room's id. A watcher not
connected to the app still needs the room id to derive its topics and key.
Use a password or a separate `app` for a room that must be private.

*A shared connection exposes material for password guesses.* A peer
connected through another room reaches a protected room's handshake by
presence without knowing its key. It cannot join with another password.
It can choose a lower id, send a challenge and receive the SHA-256 of
`<challenge>:<password>:<app>:<room>`, enough for an offline password guess.
The responder answers one challenge per binding; a lower-id peer can
rebind it at its own pace, so the number of answers is unbounded, and one
suffices. Trystero JS has the same exposure; closing it needs a wire change
shared with Trystero.

*One identity links the app's rooms.* Each room announces the same
`peerId` in clear, and connected peers see it across shared rooms. Relay
observers and peers can link those rooms to one program. Separate apps
provide separate identities and connections for rooms that must remain
unlinked. A forged announcement in a connected peer's name binds nothing:
only the peer itself, on its encrypted connection, can advertise the token.
It causes no report or rebinding count against that innocent peer.

*Two transient cases remain with a Trystero JS lower id.* If it binds by
reuse during the higher {cpp} id's dropping window, its challenge is
dropped and its handshake can time out once. With the default 10 s timeout
the next rebinding joins by at most 43 s after the first kick plus one-way
delay, with no false password error on either side. If a JS page's timer
that expires a stale answer runs later than the 1 s margin before presence
arrives, JS can flush the old answer into a new handshake and report a
false password error. One-minute timer lag can repeat this at the 11, 22
and 44 s steps, at most three times, before the 88 s step exceeds that lag.
The {cpp} side reports nothing false in either case.
