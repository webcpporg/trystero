[#trystero-protocol]
== The protocol

A room keeps Trystero 0.26.0's wire byte for byte. The protocol's pure
functions are in `<webcpp/trystero/protocol/>`; their tests compare them,
byte for byte, with values Trystero JS computed itself
(<<trystero-testing>>). What a Trystero JS peer or a page sees never changes:
the topic prefixes `Trystero@` and `Trystero:`, the handshake's
`+__trystero_pw+`, every constant and message layout, and the default relays
in their order.

[#trystero-protocol-discovery]
=== Discovery over Nostr

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Part | What it is

| Root topic
| Trystero's `sha1` of `Trystero@<app>@<room>`: each SHA-1 byte in base 36,
  unpadded, concatenated

| Peer topic
| the same of `Trystero@<app>@<room>@<peer id>`, where a peer receives its
  offers, answers and candidates

| Event
| kind 20000 plus the sum of the topic's UTF-16 code units modulo 10000,
  ephemeral; the topic in an `x` tag; signed with BIP-340

| Subscription
| one per relay, to the root topic and the peer's own topic

| Announcement
| `+{"peerId":...,"nonce":...}+` on the root topic: at once, then after 233,
  533, 1333 and 5333 ms, then every 60 s

| Relays
| the configured ones, or 5 of Trystero's 29 defaults, shuffled with
  `sin(seed{plus}{plus}) * 10000` seeded by the app id
|===

A relay that answers `rate-limited:` is skipped for 60 s, doubling to 15
minutes while the replies persist; one that answers `blocked:`,
`restricted:`, `auth-required:` or `pow:` is retired. A lost relay is
reconnected after a random share of 3.3 s, doubling to 60 s, and the delay
comes back to 3.3 s only once a connection stayed open for 60 s, so that a
relay that closes the room at once after each open is tried less and less
often.

[#trystero-protocol-signaling]
=== Signaling

Of two peers, the one with the lower id offers, on the relay that carried the
other's announcement. The higher one answers an announcement by announcing
itself on the other's peer topic, so the lower one offers: at most once a
second per peer, and once in 5.3 s per relay for the whole room, Trystero's
announce interval, so that a flood of low ids does not have the relays
rate-limit the room. Offers, answers and candidates are sealed with AES-GCM
under the SHA-256 of `<password>:<app>:<room>`, the password empty when there
is none, with a 16-byte IV, as the text `<iv>$<ciphertext>`: the IV's bytes
in decimal joined by commas, and the ciphertext with its tag in base64.
Candidates follow their offer or answer, trickled. A negotiation ends when
the data channel opens: the connection is the room's, and signals from that
peer are ignored until it leaves.

[#trystero-protocol-channel]
=== The data channel

Every message is a room frame: `1`, the length of the room token (16 bits,
big-endian), the token, the hexadecimal SHA-256 of `Trystero:<app>:<room>`,
then an action frame. A presence frame, `2`, present or not, the token's
length and the token, announces or withdraws the room.

Action frames are Trystero's action wire, version 2: an inline frame carries
the payload whole; a larger payload is an offer, accepted or refused by the
receiver, then chunks of at most 16,370 bytes, in order. Integers and
float64 values are big-endian, as JavaScript's `DataView` writes them.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Frame | Bytes

| inline (kind 0) | 2, the kind and the format shifted by four, the name (32 bytes, NUL padded), the metadata's length (uint16), the metadata, the payload
| offer (kind 1) | the inline header, the transfer's id (uint32), the payload's size (float64), the metadata
| chunk (kind 2) | 2, 2, the transfer's id (uint32), the offset (float64), the bytes
| accepted (kind 3) | 2, 3, the transfer's id (uint32)
| rejected (kind 4) | 2, 4, the transfer's id (uint32), the reason (UTF-8, at most 200 bytes)
| cancelled (kind 5) | 2, 5, the transfer's id (uint32)
|===

The payload's format is text (0), JSON (1) or binary (2). Internal actions
run the room:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Action | What it carries

| `@_hsdata` | the password handshake: the lower id sends a challenge, and the other answers with the hexadecimal SHA-256 of `<challenge>:<password>:<app>:<room>`
| `@_hsready` | the sender's side of the handshake passed; a peer joins when both sides sent it
| `@_ping`, `@_pong` | round trips
| `@_leave` | the sender leaves the room
|===

The media actions, `@_stream`, `@_track` and `@_signal`, are not ported
(<<trystero-differences>>): a frame of one is ignored.

[#trystero-protocol-bounds]
=== Bounded signaling

Announcements are not sealed, so anyone who knows the app and room ids can
announce any number of peer ids, and anyone who reads the relays can send any
signal in any peer's name. Trystero JS bounds none of this; trystero bounds
what each costs a room.

* Nothing a peer sends changes a negotiation before it opens with the room
  key: an offer, an answer or a candidate that does not open is counted,
  reported once per negotiation, and changes nothing else. A flood of fresh
  ids makes each a negotiation, so the reports are paced: the first of a
  quiet 10 s goes to `on_error` at once, with its peer, and the others of
  those 10 s in one report, with no peer and their number.
* A room holds at most `detail::signaling::max_unconnected_peers` (32)
  negotiations with peers it is not connected to. A negotiation whose offer
  and answer expire without a connection is dropped, an idle one gives its
  place to a new peer, and a new peer beyond the bound is refused and logged
  once.
* A room makes at most `max_connections_per_window` (64) connections in any
  57 s, however soon they end, and a page at most 256
  (<<trystero-browser-bounds>>); the native backend makes at most 256 a
  minute (<<trystero-native-config>>).
* A signal's fields are bounded in `protocol/signal.hpp`: the peer id (64
  bytes; Trystero's are 20), the offer id (64 bytes), a sealed offer or
  answer (64 KiB) and a sealed candidate (16 KiB, above the 8192 bytes of
  plaintext Trystero takes).
* A relay is a third party too: its texts are bounded at 1 MiB, what the room
  holds unsent for it at 4 MiB, and, natively, its silence at 30 s
  (<<trystero-native-relays>>).

[#trystero-protocol-replay]
=== Replayed offers and answers

A sealed offer or answer holds neither its offer id nor a peer id
(Trystero's wire), so one recorded from the relays still opens when anyone
resends it in its sender's name, and so does the room's own, sent back to it.
A room remembers, by the SHA-256 of their opened text, the last 1024 offers
and answers it made or acted on
(`detail::signaling::remembered_descriptions`), and refuses one that comes to
it as a peer's: it changes nothing and is logged. It makes or acts on at most
two per connection it makes, so each is remembered for at least an offer's
lifetime (57 s), and in a quiet room for its whole life. So a room refuses a
sealed offer or answer it acted on, resent, and one it made itself, sent back
as a peer's offer or answer.

[#trystero-protocol-limitations]
=== What remains

Two limitations remain, both of Trystero's wire, which only a change that
Trystero JS shares can close.

*A watcher without the key can keep two peers apart.* A sealed offer or
answer the room neither made nor acted on still opens and is acted on. Such
texts are not scarce: every peer seals a fresh offer for any id announced
above its own, and announcements need no key. So anyone who reads and
publishes on the relays, without the key, can keep any two peers of a room
apart from their first meeting for as long as it keeps at it, each attempt
costing the other peer a connection of its window. It cannot read or alter
anything the peers exchange, join the room, or touch peers already joined; a
joined peer that drops is a new peer again. The room refuses only what it
made or already acted on. Only a wire that seals the offer id and both peer
ids closes this, which Trystero would have to share (reported upstream).

*A flooder can delay new peers.* Joined peers, memory and connections stay
safe under a flood, but a flooder who keeps sending new ids takes each place,
or each connection of the window, as it frees, so new peers can be kept out
for as long as the flood lasts, each place up to one offer's lifetime (57 s)
at a time. A joined peer whose connection drops comes back as a new peer,
with the same id, and is kept out the same way: the room has no
renegotiation that would keep its place. Closing it needs announcements that
prove the room key, a wire change Trystero would have to share.
