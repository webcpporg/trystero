[#trystero-protocol]
== The protocol

A room speaks Trystero 0.27.0's topics, room keys, sealing, ids, identities,
handshake, relays, Nostr events and action wire, byte for byte. The
protocol's pure functions are in `<webcpp/trystero/protocol/>`; the
`crypto_layer` test compares them, byte for byte, with the vectors 0.27.0's
own code computed, `test/fixtures/expected/trystero-0.27.0.json`, which
`test/oracle/record_0_27.mjs` records by importing 0.27.0's
`packages/core/src` and `packages/nostr/src` unchanged and pinning
randomness only around the calls whose output it decides, and keeping the
WebCrypto signatures of its previous output that 0.27.0 still verifies; the
`protocol` and `signaling` tests hold, against the values Trystero 0.27.0
writes for the oracle lane, a Nostr event's id, the JSON texts and the
signals 0.27.0 refuses (<<trystero-testing>>).

[#trystero-protocol-crypto]
=== Topics, ids, room keys and sealing

Every value below is 0.27.0's, as its `crypto.ts`, `primitives.ts` and
`utils.ts` compute it; the vectors hold each for the app `example.com/app`
and the room `lobby`, the names written raw in UTF-8 as `JSON.stringify`
writes them, `café` and an emoji among the vectors.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Value | What it is

| Path and topic
| `topic_path` writes its parts as `JSON.stringify` writes an array of
  strings; `hash_topic` is the base64url SHA-256 of a text's UTF-8 bytes.
  The root topic is `hash_topic(["Trystero",<app>,<room>])`, and a peer's
  topic `hash_topic([<root path>,<peer id>])`, over the root topic's path
  and the peer's id, where it receives its offers, answers and candidates

| Domain tags
| `trystero:<name>:v<version>`, `domain_tag`, which separates each use of a
  key or a hash from every other

| `hash32` and `str_to_num`
| FNV-1a 32 over the UTF-8 bytes, then MurmurHash3's `fmix32`; `str_to_num`
  is `hash32` modulo its limit, `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` by default

| Ids
| `gen_id(n)` draws `ceil(n * 1.15) + 4` bytes a round and keeps
  `0-9A-Za-z[b % 62]` of each byte below 248, skipping the others, until `n`
  characters: peer ids of 20, user ids of 22, offer ids of 12, nonces of 8,
  subscription ids of 64. `uuid_v4` is RFC 9562's version 4 over 16 random
  bytes. A peer's id and a user's are derived from a key
  (<<trystero-protocol-identities>>)

| Base64url
| RFC 4648's URL alphabet without padding; `from_base64url` accepts a text
  only when it is nonempty, holds only `[A-Za-z0-9_-]`, has no padding and
  writes the same text again from its bytes, as `fromBase64Url` and its
  re-encoding check accept it

| Room key without a password
| AES-256 keyed with the SHA-256 of `["trystero:room-aes:v3",<app>,<room>]`

| Room keys with a password
| a PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA-256 master of the password's UTF-8, 600,000 iterations,
  32 bytes, under the salt SHA-256 of
  `["trystero:pbkdf2-master:v3",<app>,<room>]`, derived off the room's
  thread (<<trystero-design-port>>); then HKDF-SHA-256 with 32 zero bytes
  of salt to the AES key, info `trystero:room-aes:v3`, and the HMAC key,
  info `trystero:room-hmac:v3`

| Room auth token
| the base64url HMAC-SHA-256 of
  `["trystero:ov-auth:v3",<app>,<room>,<peer id>,<seq>]`; verification
  refuses a token that is not strict base64url of 32 bytes, or a `seq` past
  2^53^ - 1, and compares the MACs in constant time
  (`webcpp::crypto::equal`). The port computes and verifies it, and sends
  none: 0.27.0 uses it for `scale()`, which is not ported

| Sealed text
| AES-256-GCM with a 12-byte IV drawn from the backend's randomness and the
  AAD, written as the base64url of `iv \|\| ciphertext \|\| tag`; `open` refuses
  a text that is not strict base64url, one shorter than 28 bytes, a tag that
  does not match, and a plaintext that is not UTF-8 (<<trystero-differences>>)

| A signal's AAD
| `<type>\|<peer id>\|<offer id>`: `offer`, `answer` or `candidate`, the
  sender, and the offer the signal belongs to
|===

[#trystero-protocol-identities]
=== Identities

Every key of 0.27.0's identities is ECDSA P-256 (`identity.ts`), and so is
every signature, over the SHA-256 of what it signs: 64 bytes, r then s, the
form WebCrypto writes, always written with a low S and refused with a high
one (`protocol/ecdsa.hpp`), sent as base64url.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Value | What it is

| Compact key
| A public key as `base64url(x) + "." + base64url(y)`, 87 characters;
  `parse_compact_public_key` refuses another length, a misplaced `.`, a
  half that is not strict base64url of 32 bytes, and a point not on the
  curve

| Peer id
| `derive_peer_id`: the low 20 base62 digits of the SHA-256 of
  `trystero:pid:v1:<compact key>`, over the app's endpoint key, which the
  app makes when it is made; it is the app's `selfId` in every room

| User id
| `derive_user_id`: the low 22 base62 digits of the SHA-256 of
  `trystero:uid:v1:<compact key>`, over the user's key

| A user key's text
| `trystero_uk_` and the base64url of the JSON `{"d":..,"x":..,"y":..}`,
  the private scalar and the point, each the base64url of 32 bytes, which
  `export_user_identity` writes; `import_user_identity` also reads the JSON
  of a private P-256 JWK, and accepts a key only when it signs the bytes of
  `trystero:identity-self-test:v1` with a signature its point verifies, as
  `importUserIdentity` does. The text holds the secret key

| A user's proof of an endpoint
| `protocol::user_proof_payload`, 0.27.0's `userEndpointProofPayload`,
  which its core neither sends nor checks; kept for the plugins that may
  (design D21)
|===

[#trystero-protocol-discovery]
=== Discovery over Nostr

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Part | What it is

| Topics
| the root topic and the peer's own (<<trystero-protocol-crypto>>)

| Event
| kind 20000 plus FNV-1a 32 over the topic's UTF-16 code units, without
  `fmix32`, modulo 10000, ephemeral; the topic in an `x` tag; signed with
  BIP-340 by a key of the room's own, made at its first event
  (webcpp::crypto's `curves::secp256k1`)

| Subscription
| on each relay, the peer's own topic then the root topic, at most 250
  topics a REQ, with the deduplicated kinds of its topics and `since` a
  minute ago, `max(0, now - 60)`

| Announcement
| `+{"peerId":...,"nonce":...}+` on the root topic, with a fresh nonce of 8
  characters each time: at once, then after 233, 533, 1333 and 5333 ms, then
  every 60 s; an announcement a relay cannot take now is skipped, and the
  cadence goes on

| Relays
| the configured ones, or `relay_redundancy` (5) of 0.27.0's 30 defaults,
  in the order of its seeded shuffle: a 32-bit generator,
  `state += 0x6d2b79f5` and its two mixing steps, seeded with
  `str_to_num(<app>)`, in a Fisher-Yates from the end
|===

A relay that answers `rate-limited:` cools down for 60 s, doubling to 15
minutes while the replies persist, then is on probation, which an accepted
event ends; one that answers `blocked:`, `restricted:`, `auth-required:` or
`pow:` is retired and closed, and the room goes on over its other relays. A
CLOSED of a subscription not the room's own is ignored; a closed
subscription is asked again after 5333 ms, doubling to 60 s, or after the
cooldown. When the last pending EOSE comes 1 s or more after its REQ, the
announcements are replayed. A lost relay is reconnected after a random share
of 3.3 s, doubling to 60 s, and the delay comes back to 3.3 s only once a
connection stayed open for 60 s, so that a relay that closes the room at
once after each open is tried less and less often; a reopened relay is
subscribed again and its announcement replayed once, without a new warm-up.
When the first connection of every relay closed before any opened, the room
reports `relay_failed` once. `app::set_relay_connectivity` closes every
relay of the app's rooms and holds them closed (`offline`), reconnects at
once each relay not connected (`online`), or follows the page's `online`
and `offline` events (`automatic`, online natively).

A relay may repeat an event, and every relay of the room carries it, so a
room acts on an event once: it remembers the ids of the newest 4096 events it
received (`detail::nostr_strategy::remembered_events`) and drops one it
remembers. An id is whatever a relay sends, so the room remembers one only
once it has checked that it is the event's own, the SHA-256 of the event's
NIP-01 serialization (`protocol::nostr::has_own_id`), and acts on an event
whose id is not its own each time it comes, as Trystero acts on every event:
a relay cannot have the room drop a genuine event by sending a forged one
under its id first. The room indexes the ids with SipHash under a key it
draws when it is made: no relay can choose ids that fall together, as it can
in the standard library's unseeded hash, where each lookup would walk all of
them.

[#trystero-protocol-signaling]
=== Signaling

Of two peers, the one with the lower id offers, on the relay that carried the
other's announcement. The higher one answers an announcement by announcing
itself on the other's peer topic, so the lower one offers: at most once a
second per peer, and once in 5.3 s per relay for the whole room, Trystero's
announce interval, so that a flood of low ids does not have the relays
rate-limit the room. Offers, answers and candidates are sealed under the
room's AES key, derived from the immutable `app_config::password`, or
without one when it is empty, with the AAD of their type, their sender and
their offer id, and opened in the call (<<trystero-protocol-crypto>>).
Candidates follow their offer or answer, trickled. A negotiation ends when
the data channel opens: the room's link to that peer takes the connection
and its channel, which no other room uses, and the handshake starts on it.
A signal 0.27.0's `parseSignalEnvelope` refuses is ignored
(<<trystero-protocol-bounds>>).

[#trystero-protocol-handshake]
=== The handshake

Every frame of a room travels bare on its connection to the peer: an action
frame of the wire below, with no envelope, as 0.27.0's `sendFrame` writes
it. Before it delivers any other, a room runs 0.27.0's identity handshake
with the peer (`handshake.ts`), whose messages are payloads of the internal
action `@_hsdata`, JSON texts of at most 64 KiB:

The initiator is the side whose id is lower in UTF-16 order, as 0.27.0
compares its strings:

[cols="1,2,8",options="header"]
|===
| Step | Sender | What it is

| 1
| The initiator
| `{"__trystero_id":1,"n":..,"dtls":[..],` `"uid":..,"pk":..,"uk":..}`: its
  nonce, a version 4 UUID drawn for the run; the DTLS fingerprints of its
  description; its user id; its compact endpoint and user keys

| 2
| The responder
| The same members of its own, then `"ps":..,"us":..`: its endpoint's and
  its user's signatures of the transcript

| 3
| The initiator
| `{"__trystero_id":3,"ps":..,"us":..}`, once step 2 verified: its own two
  signatures of the transcript
|===

The transcript is the JSON array
`["trystero:id-hs:v2",<app>,<room>,<initiator>,<responder>,<initiator nonce>,<responder nonce>,[<initiator dtls>],[<responder dtls>],<initiator user>,<responder user>]`,
as `JSON.stringify` writes it, so that the signatures bind the app, the
room, both peers, both runs and both users to this connection. A
description's fingerprints are the values of its `a=fingerprint:` lines,
trimmed, lowercased, deduplicated and sorted (`dtls_fingerprints`), read
from the last local description the signaling set and the last remote one
it applied. Each side checks that a step's `pk` derives the peer's id, that
its `uk` derives its `uid`, that the fingerprints it claims are those of
the description this side applied, and that both signatures verify, through
the app's key records and verified signatures (design D18). A step of
another shape, a nonce shorter than 16 UTF-16 code units, or a signature
that is not strict base64url fails as "peer identity verification failed".

Once its steps passed, each side sends `@_hsready`: an empty text, or, from
a room with `max_users` that cannot take the peer's user,
`{"ok":false,"err":"room full"}`; a Trystero JS peer with roles may send
`{"ok":false,"err":"peer banned"}`, with a reason. The peer joins once both
sides sent an empty one: the ready frame goes before the join is reported,
so that a send in `on_peer_join` follows it on the wire. A handshake fails
after 10 s ("handshake timed out after 10000ms"), at a 17th message the
handshake has not read ("too many pending handshake messages"), or for a
step's reason, and its room reports `handshake_failed` with that reason,
without 0.27.0's `Trystero:` prefix, then tells the peer to leave. While a
peer is pending, only its `@_hsdata`, `@_hsready` and `@_leave` are read;
its other frames are not delivered.

[#trystero-protocol-json]
=== JSON texts

Every JSON text a room reads or writes is webcpp::document's. A text is read
by `webcpp::document::parse` into a `webcpp::document::pmr::document` of the
room's resource, or of the app's for a candidate, which holds its image and
the parse's working state, and read through its handles as views; a key
written twice keeps its last value, as `JSON.parse` keeps it. Each caller
bounds what it reads, as `parse_options::max_bytes`, and drops a longer text
before reading it, as it drops a malformed one:

[cols="2,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Text | Bound | Why

| a relay's text | 68,608 bytes | an event that carries the longest signal, with 2 KiB for its other fields
| a signal | 66,560 bytes | a sealed offer or answer of 64 KiB with the longest ids, and 1 KiB to spare
| a handshake message | 65,536 bytes | 0.27.0's own bound of an `@_hsdata` payload, `maxPayloadBytes`
| a remote candidate | 4,096 bytes | the bound of a sealed candidate, which its opened JSON never passes
|===

The image grows in place, twice as large each time, to at most twice the
bound and 64 KiB (`protocol::json::protocol_limits`, as
`parse_options::max_document_bytes`): a text Trystero writes holds a few dozen
values, and a text of thousands of values, which would need a hundred times
its length, is refused once its image has grown to that bound. A protocol
text nests at most 16 deep (`protocol::json::max_protocol_depth`, as
`parse_options::max_depth`), where Trystero's nest 4 deep at most, so that a
hostile relay's text takes the parse's recursion no deeper. A document keeps
no hashed index, so no key read twice, or chosen to collide, makes a parse
slower than the logarithm of its object's size: no text costs a room more
than a multiple of its bound to read.

A JSON payload, received as either document type, is read into an image of
at most 96 times its length and 64 KiB (`protocol::json::payload_limits`):
each value of a document is a node of 192 bytes, and one of a single
character takes two bytes of text with its comma, so every valid payload
fits. The room's `room_options::max_json_document_bytes`, 64 MiB by default,
bounds that image whatever the payload, so that a program bounds what a
peer's JSON may take of its room's resource: some 350,000 values of one
character pass the default (<<trystero-differences-json-refused>>). A
payload's other bounds are the room's `max_receive_bytes` and document's
defaults, values nested at most 256 deep; and document refuses a lone
surrogate, `"\ud800"`, which `JSON.parse` takes. A payload past any of them
is not delivered.

A parse recurses once a level of nesting. Under Asyncify, the build leaves
Boost.JSON's recursive descent, the `+parse_*+` functions of its
`basic_parser`, out of Asyncify's instrumentation (`asyncify-remove.json`, which
`/webcpp/trystero//trystero` links), since none of them suspends: instrumented,
a level took some 6.5 KB of the engine's stack in a debug build before the
engine optimized the code, so a peer's first payload nested 256 deep
overflowed node's stack at about 150 levels; uninstrumented, it reaches 400,
and in a release build more than 1,000. The room test sends such a payload
first, in both suspension variants.

A text the protocol writes is a fixed shape of a few strings and numbers,
appended field by field, its strings quoted by `webcpp::document::quote`, the
bytes `JSON.stringify` writes. A string that is not UTF-8 cannot be quoted, and
the writer returns that error: a program gives no string that is ever
written, the peer id being derived, and a local candidate whose text is not
UTF-8, which no browser's is, is not sent.

[#trystero-protocol-channel]
=== The data channel

Every message on a room's channel is an action frame, bare, as 0.27.0's
`peer-transport.ts` writes it: no envelope names the room, since the
connection is the room's alone. Before the room binds the peer, once the
channel opens, its link keeps at most 64 frames of at most 17 KiB, for at
most 10 s, as 0.27.0's `peer.ts` keeps them; one more, a longer one, or the
deadline passed ends the link. Once bound, a frame of more than 64 KiB ends
it. A connection whose state becomes `failed` or `closed` ends its link at
once, and one that is `disconnected` after 5 s unless it comes back. A frame
or a chunk is handed to the channel only while it buffers at most 64 KiB,
waiting at most 10 s for it to drain; inline sends have no rate bound of
trystero's.

Action frames are 0.27.0's action wire, version 3 (`action-wire.ts`), as the
frames the recorder captured from it show: an inline frame carries the
payload whole; a larger payload is an offer, accepted or refused by the
receiver, then chunks of at most 16,370 bytes, in order, or, when the offer
carries the wide flag, 64, of at most 65,522 bytes, 64 KiB with their
header. As 0.27.0, a room offers a transfer wide when its peer's connection
takes SCTP messages of 64 KiB at least, the backend's
`peer_connection::max_message_size` (`sctp.maxMessageSize`), read as the
offer is sent, and narrow otherwise or when the connection says no size;
it receives either. Integers and
float64 values are big-endian, as JavaScript's `DataView` writes them. An
offer's size is a float64 that must be a whole number from 1 to 2^53^ - 1,
JavaScript's `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` (`protocol::max_offer_size`), as
Trystero checks it: any other makes the frame malformed, and the room
reports `peer_error` and tells its sender to leave, as Trystero does. The size is read in 64 bits on every target,
so a 32-bit one too compares the whole size with `max_receive_bytes`, and
refuses a transfer beyond it as Trystero does.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Frame | Bytes

| inline (kind 0) | 3, the kind and the format shifted by four, the name (32 bytes, NUL padded), the metadata's length (uint16), the metadata, the payload
| offer (kind 1) | the inline header, with the wide flag, 64, in its second byte when its chunks are wide; the transfer's id (uint32), the payload's size (float64), the metadata
| chunk (kind 2) | 3, 2, the transfer's id (uint32), the offset (float64), the bytes
| accepted (kind 3) | 3, 3, the transfer's id (uint32)
| rejected (kind 4) | 3, 4, the transfer's id (uint32), the reason (UTF-8, at most 200 bytes)
| cancelled (kind 5) | 3, 5, the transfer's id (uint32)
|===

A receiver keeps at most 1024 offers pending across its peers and 64 of one
peer, reserves at most `max_receive_bytes` across them, and drops a peer
whose channel buffers more than 1 MiB when a control frame is to be sent to
it, as 0.27.0 does. A frame of an action the room has not made is dropped,
and its offer rejected at once as an "unregistered action", as 0.27.0 keeps
none: a page that makes an action after it joined misses what came before.
A frame of an action made, before the action listens, waits for it, 32 per
action and peer, the oldest dropped first, for 10 s.

The payload's format is text (0), JSON (1) or binary (2). Internal actions
run the room:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Action | What it carries

| `@_hsdata` | a step of the identity handshake, a JSON text of at most 64 KiB (<<trystero-protocol-handshake>>)
| `@_hsready` | the sender's side of the handshake passed, an empty text, or its refusal of the receiver, `{"ok":false,"err":"room full"}` or `{"ok":false,"err":"peer banned"}` with a reason; a peer joins when both sides sent an empty one
| `@_ping` | a ping, a request of the internal action `@_ping` with an empty text, answered through `@_response` with an empty text
| `@_leave` | the sender leaves the room
| `@_response` | the answer to a request: a request is a message of its action whose metadata is `{"r":<id>,"m":<metadata>}`, and its answer a message of `@_response` whose metadata is `{"r":<id>}`, or `{"r":<id>,"e":<reason>}` for a refusal, its reason cut at 512 UTF-16 code units, as 0.27.0's
|===

The media actions, `@_stream`, `@_track` and `@_signal`, are not ported
(<<trystero-differences>>): a frame of one is ignored.

[#trystero-protocol-bounds]
=== Bounded signaling

Announcements are not sealed, so anyone who knows the app and room ids can
announce any number of peer ids, and anyone who reads the relays can send any
signal in any peer's name. Trystero JS bounds none of this; trystero bounds
what each costs a room.

* Nothing a peer sends changes a negotiation before it opens with the room
  key: an offer, an answer or a candidate that does not open is counted and
  changes nothing else. An offer or an answer is reported to `on_error` as
  `decrypt_failed`, with its peer and stage, at most once a minute per peer
  and stage, the last 512 remembered, as 0.27.0 reports them; a candidate
  is not reported.
* A room holds at most `detail::signaling::max_unconnected_peers` (32)
  negotiations with peers it is not connected to. A negotiation whose offer
  and answer expire without a connection is dropped, an idle one gives its
  place to a new peer, and a new peer beyond the bound is refused and logged
  once. A negotiation is idle when it holds no connection: the one idle
  longest gives its place, and of those idle as long, as every negotiation
  is at one instant of a page's coarse clock, the one the room took first.
  A signal opens in the call that reads it (<<trystero-design-port>>), so
  no offer waits, unopened, for its place.
* A negotiation answers one offer of its peer at a time, as Trystero answers
  one at a time: while it answers one, another offer in that name is
  ignored, and the same offer through another relay has the answer sent
  there too. So offers sent in one name, however many, hold one answer. As
  with Trystero, an earlier offer of the peer's, resent by a watcher and
  answered first, has the peer's own, arriving meanwhile, wait until the
  peer sends it again, 2 s later.
* A room makes at most `max_connections_per_window` (64) connections in any
  57 s, however soon they end; the platform backend makes at most 256 a
  minute on both targets (<<trystero-platform-config>>), and a page holds at
  most 256 (<<trystero-platform-runtime>>).
* A signal is read as 0.27.0's `parseSignalEnvelope` reads it
  (`protocol::read_signal`), and refused where it refuses it: a peer id of
  other than 20 base62 characters, an offer id of other than 1 to 64
  characters of `[\w.~:!-]` (or none with an offer, answer or candidate), a
  sealed offer or answer of more than 64 Ki UTF-16 code units, a sealed
  candidate of more than 4,096. The vectors of `signal_envelopes` in
  `test/fixtures/oracle/expected.json` show 0.27.0 refusing each. 0.27.0
  parses a text of up to 128 Ki characters; a room drops one of more than
  66,560 bytes unread, which only members Trystero never writes, or sealed
  text that is not ASCII and never opens, can make.
* A relay is a third party too: its texts are bounded at 1 MiB, what the room
  holds unsent for it at 4 MiB, and, natively, its silence at 30 s
  (<<trystero-platform-relays>>).

[cols="2,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Bound | Scope | Value and consequence

| Frames before the room binds a peer
| Connection
| 64, each at most 17 KiB, for 10 s after the first, as 0.27.0's `peer.ts`;
  one more, a longer one, or the time passed ends the connection.

| A frame once bound
| Connection
| 64 KiB, as 0.27.0; a longer one ends the connection.

| Handshake
| Room and peer
| 10 s, as 0.27.0, which has no option for it, and 16 messages it has not
  read; failure ends that peer only.

| Users
| Room
| `room_options::max_users`, none by default; a peer of a new user is
  refused past it, a peer of a user present is admitted.

| Key records and verified signatures
| App
| 4,096 records, and 8,192 signatures of payloads of at most 4,096 bytes,
  2 MiB in all, as 0.27.0's caches; the least recently used out.

| Action frames before a listener
| Action and peer
| 32 for 10 s, of an action made; of an action not made, none.

| Transfers and queued messages
| Room and peer
| 1024 pending offers, 64 a peer, `max_receive_bytes` across peers, and
  1024 queued messages per action once it is awaited.
|===

Each room negotiates its own connection to each peer, as 0.27.0's rooms
do: an app whose rooms meet one peer in three rooms holds three connections
to it, each counted in its room's window of 64 connections in 57 s, and
each closed as its room leaves or the peer goes.

[#trystero-protocol-replay]
=== Replayed offers and answers

A sealed offer or answer is bound by its AAD to its type, its sender and its
offer id (<<trystero-protocol-crypto>>), so one recorded from the relays
opens only as what it was: never as another's, nor an answer sent back as an
offer. Resent so, in its sender's name and for its offer, it still opens,
and so does the room's own, sent back to it under its own id. A room
remembers, by the SHA-256 of their opened text, the last 1024 offers
and answers it made or acted on
(`detail::signaling::remembered_descriptions`), and refuses one that comes to
it as a peer's: it changes nothing and is logged. It makes or acts on at most
two per connection it makes, so each is remembered for at least an offer's
lifetime (57 s), and in a quiet room for its whole life. So a room refuses a
sealed offer or answer it acted on, resent, and one it made itself, sent back
as a peer's offer or answer.

[#trystero-protocol-limitations]
=== What remains

Each {cpp} app is one password domain: it copies one password at
construction, and all its rooms use it. A room's signals are sealed under
keys derived from that password, so a peer of another password opens none
of them and never connects, as in 0.27.0, which has no password challenge.
There are no per-room overrides, and different passwords require separate
apps with distinct self ids.

*A watcher not connected to the app, without the key, can keep two peers
apart.* A sealed offer or answer the room neither made nor acted on still
opens and is acted on: the AAD names its sender and its offer, not its
receiver. Such texts are not scarce: every peer seals a fresh offer for any
id announced above its own, and announcements need no key. So anyone who
reads and publishes on the relays, without the key, can keep any two peers
of a room apart from their first meeting for as long as it keeps at it, each
attempt costing the other peer a connection of its window. It cannot read
or alter anything the peers exchange, join the room, or touch peers already
joined: the signed handshake that follows each connection admits only the
peer whose key derives the id it connected as, over the fingerprints of
that connection. A joined peer that drops is a new peer again. The room
refuses only what it made or already acted on. Only a wire that also seals
the receiver's id closes this, which Trystero would have to share.

*A flooder can delay new peers.* Joined peers, memory and connections stay
safe under a flood, but a flooder who keeps sending new ids takes each place,
or each connection of the window, as it frees, so new peers can be kept out
for as long as the flood lasts, each place up to one offer's lifetime (57 s)
at a time. A joined peer whose connection drops comes back as a new peer,
with the same id, and is kept out the same way: the room has no
renegotiation that would keep its place. Closing it needs announcements that
prove the room key, a wire change Trystero would have to share.

*One identity links the app's rooms.* Each room announces the same
`peerId` in clear, and its handshake presents the same endpoint and user
keys, so relay observers and peers can link the rooms of one app to one
program, as 0.27.0 links the rooms of one page. Separate apps provide
separate identities for rooms that must remain unlinked; apps given one
`user_identity` are one user's endpoints, by design.
