[#trystero-platform]
== The platform backend

`platform::backend` implements the backend interface on both targets, one
class for a native program and for a page
(`<webcpp/trystero/platform/backend.hpp>`, `/webcpp/trystero//platform`). Its
WebRTC is webcpp::webrtc's, natively
https://github.com/paullouisageneau/libdatachannel[libdatachannel]'s and in a
page the page's own `RTCPeerConnection` and `RTCDataChannel`
(<<trystero-design-backend-webrtc>>); its relays' WebSockets are
webcpp::beast's, natively Boost.Beast's own and in a page its `WebSocket`;
its timers are webcpp::asio's `steady_timer`. Two parts are the target's own,
chosen by `WEBCPP_TARGET_EMSCRIPTEN`: the sealer, natively OpenSSL's AES-GCM
and in a page `crypto.subtle`, and the source of randomness, natively
OpenSSL's generator and in a page `getentropy`, which Emscripten draws from
`crypto.getRandomValues`. Its clocks are the standard library's on both:
`std::chrono::system_clock` for Nostr's `created_at`, and
`std::chrono::steady_clock` for its timing, which Emscripten reads from the
page's `Date.now()` and `performance.now()`.

A room over it is the same room as a browser's or Trystero JS's: they meet
over the same relays and exchange the same frames. One backend serves every
app and room of the program, with separate app objects and distinct self ids
for different passwords, beside the executor it runs on, and the program runs
that executor's `io_context` itself: natively blocking on it or polling it
(<<trystero-quick-start-native>>), in a page from `main`, as webcpp::asio's
model has it (<<trystero-platform-run>>).

[#trystero-platform-config]
=== Its configuration

`platform::backend_config` is what the backend makes its peer connections,
its relays and its own state with, beside the app's `ice_servers`:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Member | What it is

| `bind_address`
| The local address every peer connection binds to, as `"127.0.0.1"`; empty
  means every interface but the loopback. Bound to the loopback, the peers
  of one machine connect with no other network, which a test wants; a peer
  bound there reaches no other machine. Natively only: a page's
  `RTCPeerConnection` cannot bind, and webcpp::webrtc refuses every
  connection a page asks it to bind, so a page leaves it empty.

| `max_peer_connections_per_minute`
| The most peer connections the backend makes in any minute, for every room
  in every app it serves together, 256: beyond, it makes none, and a room
  reports each negotiation it cannot start. A room makes at most 64 in any
  57 s however its peers behave (<<trystero-protocol-bounds>>), so the
  default lets four rooms do so at once. A connection still counts against
  its negotiating room's 57 s window after adoption by the app; presence and
  reuse make none.

| `runtime`
| What the backend's own webcpp::webrtc runtime is made with,
  `webcpp::webrtc::runtime_options`: its `max_peer_connections`, the most
  connections alive at once, 256 by default, beyond which the backend makes
  none and a room reports each negotiation it cannot start; and, natively,
  `thread_pool_size`, libdatachannel's threads, its own default when 0.
  Unused when the backend is given the program's runtime
  (<<trystero-platform-runtime>>).

| `relay_limits`
| What the relays' WebSockets tolerate of a relay that stops reading or
  answering, `platform::websocket_limits` (<<trystero-platform-relays>>).

| `memory`
| The resource of the backend's own state, the default resource unless the
  program gives one: its copy of `bind_address`, its window of constructions
  and its own runtime. It must not be null, and must outlive the backend and
  every connection of its runtime. What the backend makes for an app or a
  room is in the resource given with that call instead
  (<<trystero-allocation>>).
|===

[#trystero-platform-runtime]
=== The runtime

webcpp::webrtc's `runtime` caps the peer connections alive at once, and,
natively, holds what libdatachannel keeps for the whole process, its threads
and its global state. A backend makes one of its own, with
`backend_config::runtime`, or takes the program's: `platform::backend(ex,
runtime, config)`, or `platform::backend(ex, tls, runtime, config)` with the
program's TLS context too (<<trystero-platform-relays>>). Each of its
connections and channels keeps that runtime alive, so that one which
outlives the backend, in a handler the `io_context` still holds, never
outlives the runtime. A connection gives its place back once it is gone.

Natively a process holds one runtime at a time, since libdatachannel's state
is the process's: a program with several backends at once, or that uses
webcpp::webrtc beside trystero, makes one `webcpp::webrtc::runtime` before
its `io_context` and gives it to each.

In a page the cap counts every `RTCPeerConnection` of the page, every
program and runtime of it together, from its construction until the garbage
collector frees it, as Chromium counts them against its own 500 (measured
with headless Chrome: the 501st throws "Cannot create so many
PeerConnections", and a closed one counts until a collection):
webcpp::webrtc's census of the page counts every connection it makes, with
any runtime. The default, 256, is half of Chromium's, so that a closed
connection still counts until a collection the page cannot ask for, while
the rest of Chromium's count stays free for what the page makes without a
room.

[#trystero-platform-relays]
=== The relays

The relays are reached over webcpp::beast, on both targets with the same
session: natively over Boost.Beast's own WebSocket, in a page over its
`WebSocket`; `ws://` in the clear, `wss://` over TLS. Each connection is a
chain of Asio operations on the room's executor (resolve, connect, TLS, the
handshake, then one read after another, and one write at a time), whose
handlers carry the room's allocator, so that what Asio and Beast allocate
for them, the read buffer among it, is in the room's resource
(<<trystero-allocation>>), and a message allocates nothing outside it; in a
page the stream's own state is there too. Natively what Beast, Asio and
OpenSSL allocate of their own, which takes no allocator, stays outside it,
for each connection: their streams' state, the resolver's, the handshake's
request and response, the verify callback, their timers' waits and each
keep-alive ping's frame (<<trystero-allocation-outside>>). A write that
fails ends the connection, which reports its close at once. The WebSockets
are Beast's rather than libdatachannel's own, so the relays run on the
room's executor like everything else.

A relay's URL is read with Boost.URL, on both targets, by the same rules.
It accepts `ws://` and `wss://`, the scheme in any case; a host name, with
its escapes decoded, in lower case; an IPv4 address as a dotted quad
(`ws://127.0.0.1:7447/`), also with escaped digits, which is then an
address and not a name; an IPv6 address in brackets
(`ws://[2001:db8::1]:7447/`), whose address without them is what the
resolver looks up, and which the handshake names as it is written; and a
port up to 65535, the scheme's when the URL names none. It refuses a URL of
another scheme, `http://` and `https://` among them, which a page's
`WebSocket` alone would take as `ws://` and `wss://`; one without a host,
with userinfo or with a fragment; an IPvFuture address (`ws://[v1.x]/`);
and a host with a byte that a page's URL parser refuses in a host, escaped
or not: a space or a control character, or one of `#`, `%`, `/`, `:`,
`<`, `>`, `?`, `@`, `[`, `+\+`, `]`, `^` and `|`.

It fails closed on what a page's URL parser accepts but reads otherwise: an
IPv4 address in another form than a dotted quad, which a page reads as the
address and the system's resolver might reach under another name, as the
shorthand `127.1`, octal or hexadecimal parts (`0x7f.0.0.1`), a final dot or
a single number, and so any host whose last label is a number, which a page
reads as an address or refuses; an international domain name, which a page
turns into its A-label (`+xn--bcher-kva.example+` for `bücher.example`) and
this parser does not, with its bytes beyond ASCII escaped or not; and a tab,
a newline or a backslash anywhere in the URL, which a page drops or reads as
a slash. A refused URL makes the relay report a close, as an unreachable one
does, and the room retries it later as it would any other.

Over TLS, natively, the connection names the relay's host to the server
(SNI) when it is a name, never when it is an address, verifies the relay's
certificate whatever the verify mode of the context, and checks it against
the URL's host, a name or an address; in a page the browser does all three,
against its own authorities. A program's context made as Asio makes a
client's, `ssl::context(ssl::context::tls_client)`, whose verify mode is
`verify_none`, still has every relay verified against the authorities it
trusts, as a page's `WebSocket` always verifies; the context itself is left
as it is. By default the backend makes its TLS context with
`platform::make_tls_context()`: natively TLS 1.2 or newer, and the system's
certificate authorities, those OpenSSL's default verify paths name; in a
page a context that records only the policy the page's stream asks for, a
client that verifies its peer. A relay whose certificate does not verify is
unreachable, and the room retries it later.

On Windows OpenSSL's default verify paths are a `cert.pem` file and a
`certs` directory in its build-time directory, `SSL` under the common
program files (`C:\Program Files\Common Files\SSL` on a usual machine),
which neither Windows nor webcpp fills, and OpenSSL reads no Windows
certificate store by default: there the system's authorities are none
unless `SSL_CERT_FILE` or `SSL_CERT_DIR` names some. `make_tls_context()`
succeeds there all the same, and no `wss://` relay verifies, so a Windows
program gives the backend its own context, webcpp's embedded roots as below.

A native application that trusts other authorities makes its context and
gives it to the backend: webcpp's embedded Mozilla roots, which read no
file of the system, with `webcpp::asio::make_tls_client_context()`, or a
private authority with `webcpp::asio::make_tls_client_context(pem)`:

[source]
----
include::{library-root}/test/platform_websocket_test.cpp[tag=embedded-roots,indent=0]
----

The backend takes the context as `make_tls_client_context` returns it, a
`boost::system::result`: one that holds an error, as when OpenSSL cannot
make the backend's own, is kept, `tls_context()` returns it, and every
`wss://` relay reports a close while the `ws://` ones are still reached. No
constructor throws. `tls_context()` also gives the context itself, which an
application may configure further before the backend connects to a relay.

A relay is a third party, and the backend bounds what one can make the room
hold:

* A text longer than 1 MiB (`protocol::max_relay_message_bytes`) closes the
  connection before it is copied, in a page before it enters WebAssembly.
* A relay that stops reading is dropped once the text held unsent for it
  would pass 4 MiB (`websocket_limits::max_queued_bytes`, which is
  `protocol::max_queued_relay_bytes`): the session's own queue, and in a page
  also what its `WebSocket` holds unsent, its `bufferedAmount`, which
  webcpp::beast's `queue_limits::send` bounds to the same.
* In a page, the texts a relay sent that wait for the room's executor are
  bounded too, at webcpp::beast's `queue_limits::receive`, 4 MiB: past it
  the socket closes.
* Natively, a relay that answers the handshake with a status other than 101
  can have Boost.Beast read a body of up to 8 MiB, its default limit for a
  response, outside the room's resource, before the connection reports its
  close (measured: an 8 MiB body held about 18 MiB at its peak, as the
  string that holds it grew); it is freed then, and the room retries with
  its backoff. Beast's WebSocket stream reads the response with a parser of
  its own, inside its handshake, and none of its options, nor its handshake
  that returns the response, reaches that parser's limit, so the backend
  cannot lower it. In a page the browser makes the handshake.
* Natively, a relay that stops answering is dropped once `idle_timeout`
  (30 s) passes with its keep-alive ping unanswered: halfway through, the
  connection sends a ping. The timeout counts bytes received, and a ping
  waits for the write in flight, so a relay that reads under about 2 KB/s is
  closed during a 64 KiB text. Signaling texts are 2 to 4 KB, so a real room
  meets it only with a relay slower than about 200 B/s. A page's `WebSocket`
  cannot ping, and webcpp::beast's page stream refuses keep-alive pings, so a
  page has no idle timeout.

Each drop reports one close, and the room reconnects with its backoff
(<<trystero-protocol-discovery>>).

[#trystero-platform-randomness]
=== Randomness and sealing

The backend's randomness, the room's keys and nonces, and the IV of each
seal come from the platform's one secure source, its result checked:
OpenSSL's `RAND_bytes` natively, `getentropy` in a page. When it has none,
`random()` aborts the program, since nothing is safe without it, and a seal
fails with `error::backend_failure`.

The sealer is asynchronous on both targets, as Web Crypto is: natively it
works synchronously and posts its completion, and in a page it awaits each
Web Crypto promise with webcpp::asio's `browser::async_await`, under a key it
imports on its first operation, while the operations that arrive meanwhile
wait with a copy of their text. A completion never runs inside the call that
started it.

[#trystero-platform-exceptions]
=== Exceptions

trystero raises no exception of its own, and the platform backend works with
exceptions and without them on both targets, as the rest of trystero does.
Natively libdatachannel reports errors by throwing, but webcpp::webrtc calls
its C API, which catches what it throws and returns a code: a refused
description, candidate or channel completes with `error::backend_failure`, a
connection the platform refuses is not made, and no exception crosses into
trystero. In a page no JavaScript exception enters WebAssembly
(<<trystero-platform-javascript-exceptions>>).

`<webcpp/trystero/config.hpp>`, which every header includes first, defines
`WEBCPP_TRYSTERO_NO_EXCEPTIONS` when webcpp has no exceptions
(`WEBCPP_HAS_EXCEPTIONS` is not defined: the compiler has none, or the
program defined `WEBCPP_NO_EXCEPTIONS`); a developer may define it for
trystero alone in a build that has them. It changes nothing in trystero's
behaviour. No function of the backend throws, its constructors included: when
OpenSSL cannot make the relays' TLS context, the backend keeps the error as a
value, which `tls_context()` returns, and every `wss://` relay reports a
close, as an unreachable one does (<<trystero-platform-relays>>).

[#trystero-platform-native]
=== Natively

[#trystero-platform-threads]
==== Threads

libdatachannel delivers its events on threads of its own. Neither
webcpp::webrtc nor the backend runs a handler there: webcpp::webrtc posts each
event to the room's executor, through a weak reference, so an event that
arrives after its object is gone does nothing. Every handler of the room and
every completion runs on the executor, as in a page. An application that runs
the `io_context` on several threads gives the app a strand, shared by all its
rooms. A native program that uses the backend is built with `threading=multi`
(<<trystero-building>>).

If a transport closes during adoption, before the room obtains a binding,
the room forgets that negotiation and restarts announcements. Its later close
event cannot leave signaling marked connected with no binding.

[#trystero-platform-libdatachannel]
==== What the backend asks of libdatachannel

What libdatachannel does is webcpp::webrtc's: its C API, which works without
exceptions, automatic negotiation off, so that an offer or an answer exists
only when the signaling asks for one, as in a page, and every object it
hands out held until its owner lets it go. The backend adds:

* Its connections bind to `backend_config::bind_address`.
* A TURN URL with a user name and a credential is written
  `turn:user:credential@host`, the form libdatachannel parses
  (webcpp::webrtc's `ice_server`).
* A URL libdatachannel cannot parse, as a `stuns:` one, a scheme it does not
  know, is left out of the connection, each URL being tried alone, where a
  page's `RTCPeerConnection` refuses the whole connection.
* What libdatachannel allocates inside itself is libdatachannel's
  (<<trystero-allocation-outside>>).

[#trystero-platform-page]
=== In a page

In a page the backend is given the executor of the program's `io_context`,
which the program runs from `main`. One backend serves every app and room of
the page. Apps with different passwords use distinct self ids and share no
connection or presence. `<webcpp/trystero/platform/page.hpp>`, which builds
only on emscripten, holds what a page's program uses beside it:
`platform::page_call` and `platform::js_string`.

[#trystero-platform-run]
==== The program runs its io_context

A page's program is the shape of any program of webcpp::asio: `main` makes
the `io_context`, the backend, the app and its rooms, and calls `io.run()`,
which runs the rooms' handlers on the stack of `main` and suspends into the
browser's event loop whenever nothing is ready (webcpp::asio's run family,
in either suspension variant).

* The page's events reach {cpp} through webcpp::asio's bridge, a service of
  the `io_context`: a socket's open, message or close, a settled Web Crypto
  promise, and webcpp::webrtc's events are each posted to the `io_context`,
  and run when the run family resumes, never inside the event.
* The backend's timers are webcpp::asio's `steady_timer`, in the reactor's
  timer queue: they fire while the program runs its `io_context`.
* The program holds a work guard on the `io_context` for the page's life: a
  page that waits for its user, or for its peers, has no work of its own, and
  without the guard `run` would return.
* The page reaches {cpp} through `platform::page_call`, a function of
  webcpp::asio's bridge that the program hands to the page, and whose every
  call only posts its work to the `io_context`. Nothing calls `run`, `poll`
  or `dispatch` from a JavaScript event: while `run` is suspended, an event
  sees the `io_context` as running in this thread, and a call of the run
  family there fails at its first suspension (webcpp::asio's guide, "Who may
  call it"). Nor is a page's call an embind function: under Asyncify, while
  the `run` of `main` is suspended, an embind function returns a promise
  that never settles, and its second call aborts a debug build (measured
  with Emscripten 6.0.11).
* The handler of a `page_call` is a handler of the run, and lets no
  JavaScript error out, webcpp::asio's rule for every handler, which its
  guide gives where it says what a JavaScript error out of a handler does:
  an error that leaves it leaves `run` with it, and the program's run never
  resumes (measured with Emscripten 6.0.11 under node: the process ends with
  the error, in either suspension variant). A page may pass any value, so
  the handler checks its type before it converts it, with `isString()`,
  `isNumber()` or `typeOf()`, and reads a field only of what is neither
  `null` nor `undefined`: embind's `as<std::string>()` of a number throws a
  `BindingError`, and a field of `undefined` a `TypeError`. The handler is
  given the page's value as it is when the handler runs, not when the page
  called: a JavaScript object the page changes meanwhile is seen changed.
* A `page_call` posts each call in the resource it is given, which holds the
  call until the `io_context` runs it or is destroyed: the resource outlives
  the `io_context`. A call through a function the page kept after its
  `page_call` was destroyed, or after the `io_context` was, reaches nothing,
  and a `page_call` destroyed after its `io_context` is harmless.

So nothing runs while nothing happens, a handler runs as soon as the run
family resumes after its event, and a turn of `max_handlers_per_turn`
handlers is followed by a yield, which lets the page render. A handler's
{cpp} exception propagates out of `run` to `main`, as it does natively.

[#trystero-platform-javascript]
==== How JavaScript reaches {cpp}

Every function the page calls with an event, or with a call of its own
(`platform::page_call`), is made by webcpp::asio's bridge, for a
registration and a key that is never reused, and only posts to the
`io_context`: an object registers when it is made and removes its
registration when it is destroyed, so a late event for a destroyed object
finds nothing. The relays' sockets are webcpp::beast's, whose page stream
also scopes each event to its connection, so that an event of a socket
already dropped reaches nothing. A Web Crypto promise is awaited with
webcpp::asio's `browser::async_await`, whose settlement is posted the same
way and whose operation is outstanding work until it settles. webcpp::webrtc's
objects receive the page's WebRTC events through the same bridge. Their
JavaScript is linked into every program on emscripten by `/webcpp/asio//asio`:
a program includes no source file of trystero's.

Text that crosses into JavaScript is decoded straight from the WebAssembly
memory a view points into, `platform::js_string`, and text that comes back
is encoded straight into storage of the object's resource: no `std::string`
is made to carry either.

[#trystero-platform-javascript-exceptions]
==== No JavaScript exception enters WebAssembly

The backend makes every call into JavaScript that can throw, a `WebSocket`
constructor or `send`, a Web Crypto method, in JavaScript that catches what
it throws: webcpp::beast's page stream turns a socket that cannot be made into
an error, and its relay reports its close, and a Web Crypto call is made
inside an `async` function, so that what it throws becomes the rejection of
its promise, which the {cpp} turns into the error its interface has.
webcpp::webrtc's JavaScript catches the page's WebRTC exceptions the same
way. The bridge's functions catch what still escapes a delivery, put
WebAssembly's stack pointer back and report it, and with exceptions a {cpp}
exception is caught in {cpp}, which frees it. So a page's program may be
built with exceptions or without them, and a peer's malformed message never
stops it.

[#trystero-platform-bounds]
==== What a page bounds

* A data channel message is read by webcpp::webrtc, a text as the UTF-8 it
  holds and a binary one as its bytes, and dropped on the executor when it is
  a text, as a peer can send, or larger than any room frame
  (`protocol::max_room_frame_bytes`): neither can be a frame. webcpp::webrtc
  bounds what it reads: a message past `max_message_size` (256 KiB), or
  unread messages past `max_received_bytes` (16 MiB), close the channel.
* A relay's texts are bounded as natively, and its socket's
  `bufferedAmount` and its texts waiting for the executor too
  (<<trystero-platform-relays>>); a page has no idle timeout.
* A page makes at most `runtime_options::max_peer_connections` (256) peer
  connections across all apps and rooms it serves, counted from their
  construction until the garbage collector frees them
  (<<trystero-platform-runtime>>), and at most
  `backend_config::max_peer_connections_per_minute` (256) in any minute, as
  natively. Beyond, the rooms report each negotiation they cannot start.

Peer connections, data channels and received frames use the app's resource;
relay sockets, their events, timers and sealed signaling texts use the
room's. Presence or reuse binds another room without making another peer
connection.

[#trystero-platform-needs]
==== What a page needs

* A secure context: https, or http on `localhost` or `127.0.0.1`. Elsewhere
  `crypto.subtle`, which seals the signaling, does not exist, and every
  sealing operation fails with `error::backend_failure`.
* No threads: Emscripten's threads need a cross-origin isolated page, so
  trystero uses none. Its Asio is webcpp::asio's, built without threads when
  the program has no pthread ABI, and `main` runs it on the page's one
  thread.
* A suspension variant of webcpp::asio, which `/webcpp/trystero//platform`
  brings: `webcpp-suspend=jspi`, the default, for a browser with JavaScript
  Promise Integration, or `asyncify` for any other. The `run` of `main`
  suspends in either, and a page loads a variant its browser runs: the
  chat's site is built in one, JSPI unless the build asks for
  `webcpp-suspend=asyncify` (<<trystero-building-examples>>).
* A program that runs its `io_context` from `main` for as long as the page
  lives, under a work guard: the `io_context` must outlive every room and
  object it serves, and its handlers run only while it runs.
* Boost.JSON's and Boost.URL's definitions, which a program links from
  `/webcpp//boost_json` and `/webcpp//boost_url`, which
  `/webcpp/trystero//platform` brings and which build on emscripten as on
  every target.
