[#trystero-allocation]
== Allocation

A webcpp library lets its user choose where what it allocates comes from.
trystero does it through `std::pmr`, header-only: every allocation of a room
goes through one `std::pmr::memory_resource`, which its configuration
names.

[#trystero-allocation-resource]
=== One resource per room

`room_config::memory`, which defaults to `std::pmr::get_default_resource()`,
is the resource every allocation of the room goes through, from its
construction to its destruction: the room itself, the copy of its
configuration, its peers, actions, queues, frames, transfers, negotiations
and relays, its handlers, the text it composes for its log, the objects its
backend makes for it, the context libsecp256k1 signs with
(`secp256k1_context_preallocated_create`, in the resource), and the JSON it
parses and writes. A message an action's handler receives as a
`std::pmr::string`, a `pmr_bytes` or a `boost::json::value` is there too.
trystero touches the resource only from the room's executor, so it need not
be thread-safe when that executor runs on one thread. A null `memory` is a
programming error, which `BOOST_ASSERT` catches when the room is
constructed.

trystero's allocation test makes a configuration whose every part is in the
resource it names: the strings and the relays' URLs are made in it, and the
log sink is a `handler` allocated there:

[source]
----

----

[#trystero-allocation-lifetime]
=== How long the resource lives

The resource must outlive the room, every value the room handed out (a
`std::pmr::string`, a `pmr_bytes` or a `boost::json::value` a handler kept),
and every operation the room left pending: destroying a room closes
everything, but what it closed still has operations queued that free into
the resource. On Asio, the resource lives until the `io_context` of the
room's executor has run out of the room's work or has been destroyed, so it
is declared before the `io_context`, which is then destroyed first: the
resource, then the `io_context`, then the room, in that order of
declaration. In the browser, the resource lives until the room's pending
promises have settled, since a promise's registration with the page's
`event_loop` keeps its handler, in the room's resource; in practice, for the
page's life.

No check in debug builds catches the misuse: trystero holds a raw pointer
and cannot see the resource end, and the use comes after the room is gone,
from an operation of Asio's or of the browser's. A resource that asserts
nothing is out when it is destroyed, as the test's counting resources do, is
the check, and it is the user's.

A room allocates and frees on every event, a frame, a parsed text, a post or
a log line, so a resource that reuses what is freed, a pool such as
`std::pmr::unsynchronized_pool_resource`, suits a long-lived room; a
`std::pmr::monotonic_buffer_resource` grows with every event and suits a
short-lived one, or a test.

[#trystero-allocation-completions]
=== Completion handlers

A completion handler is the user's, and is allocated as its own associated
allocator says: the room never binds its allocator over it.
`boost::asio::bind_allocator` gives one the room's resource. The allocation
test makes the allocator of a resource:

[source]
----
include::{library-root}/test/allocation_test.cpp[tag=allocator,indent=0]
----

and binds it over the completions of a transfer and of a ping, so that they
too are allocated in the sender's resource:

[source]
----
include::{library-root}/test/allocation_test.cpp[tag=bind-allocator,indent=0]
----

When the room erases a completion handler's type (an
`any_completion_handler`), it records whether the handler's associated
executor is the room's, which only the concrete type tells without
allocating; such a completion runs from an operation the room posts to its
executor, allocated with the handler's allocator. One bound to another
executor is handed to that executor by Asio, whose type-erased executor wraps
it with `std::allocator`.

[#trystero-allocation-containers]
=== Containers and shared objects

Every container trystero declares is a `std::pmr` one: `std::pmr::string`
(and `peer_id`), `std::pmr::vector` (and `backend::bytes`,
`protocol::bytes` and `pmr_bytes`), `std::pmr::deque`, `std::pmr::map`,
`std::pmr::set`, `std::pmr::unordered_map` and `std::pmr::unordered_set`,
built over the room's resource; `room_config`, `send_options`,
`message_info` and `backend::ice_server` hold them too. Two types stay the
user's choice of `std::allocator`: `trystero::bytes`, a
`std::vector<std::uint8_t>`, and `trystero::payload`, a variant of
`std::string`, `boost::json::value` and `bytes`. A received `bytes`, or the
binary of a `payload`, is a copy of the frame, which is in the room's
resource.

A struct that holds `std::pmr` containers takes the room's
`std::pmr::polymorphic_allocator<>` in its constructor, and a container of
such structs is given it when it makes one, with `try_emplace(key,
allocator)`. A `std::pmr` container is never copied without an allocator:
its copy constructor takes the default resource, so a copy names the
resource, `std::pmr::string(text, allocator)`. Moving keeps the resource.

Shared objects are made with `std::allocate_shared` over
`std::pmr::polymorphic_allocator<>`, never with `std::make_shared`,
`std::make_unique` or `new`; a timer is a `std::shared_ptr` for that reason.

[#trystero-allocation-handlers]
=== Handlers

`std::function` takes no allocator since {cpp}17, so trystero holds every
handler it keeps in `+handler<R(Args...)>+`, a move-only callable that
allocates through a `std::pmr::polymorphic_allocator<>`. Constructed with
`handler(std::allocator_arg, allocator, f)`, it holds `f` inline when `f`
fits in three pointers and is nothrow-movable, and in storage of the
allocator otherwise. It replaces every `std::function` of the backend
interface (the handlers of its timer, WebSocket, data channel, peer
connection and sealer), of the room and of the log's sink. Constructed from
`f` alone it uses the default resource; the room moves a handler it is given,
`room_config::log`, into its own resource with the allocator-extended move.
A move assignment carries the source's allocator with its target.

[#trystero-allocation-asio]
=== Asio, the backends, Boost.JSON and the protocol

*Asio.* Every `post`, `dispatch`, timer wait and `any_completion_handler`
the room makes carries `boost::asio::bind_allocator` over its resource. A
post through `boost::asio::any_io_executor` is wrapped by Asio in a function
object allocated with `std::allocator`, from Asio's per-thread recycling
cache, which calls `std::aligned_alloc` on a miss (measured: two per post,
in every round of five posts, natively and on emscripten). So the room posts
through the executor of an `io_context` when its executor is one, where Asio
allocates the operation as the handler's allocator says; on another
executor, as the browser's `event_loop` executor, the wrapper stays Asio's.

*The backends.* Each method of `backend::factory` that makes an object takes
the room's `std::pmr::memory_resource*` as its last parameter, and the
backend allocates its objects, their handlers and the frames and texts it
hands to the room through it.

*Boost.JSON* allocates through `json_resource`, a
`boost::container::pmr::memory_resource` that forwards to a
`std::pmr::memory_resource`, header-only: the global resources of
`boost::container::pmr` are compiled, and webcpp's CI installs Boost's headers
alone. The room's JSON storage is made once, when the room is constructed,
since a value a handler keeps may outlive the room. Text is parsed by a
`boost::json::parser` over that storage, never by `boost::json::parse`,
whose temporaries beyond a stack buffer come from the default resource, and
JSON is written by a `boost::json::serializer` whose stack is the resource.

*The protocol* (`protocol/`) takes a `std::pmr::memory_resource*` as the
last parameter of every function that returns what it allocates, with the
default resource as its default, and returns `std::pmr` types.
`protocol::nostr::verify` uses `secp256k1_context_static` and allocates
nothing.

[#trystero-allocation-outside]
=== What stays outside the resource

* libdatachannel's and OpenSSL's own allocations, with the `std::string` and
  `rtc::Description` libdatachannel's API takes and returns;
* what `emscripten::val` holds, in the JavaScript heap;
* the counter of the room's JSON storage, which Boost.JSON allocates with
  `operator new`, once per room;
* Asio's own once-per-context allocations: its services and the scheduler's
  first operation;
* the wrapper Asio puts around a post through an executor that is not the
  executor of an `io_context`;
* the native backend's posts from libdatachannel's threads, which carry
  Asio's default allocator, since the resource is touched only on the room's
  executor;
* Boost.Beast's and Asio's state inside the native WebSocket: its stream,
  its resolver's results, its coroutines' frames;
* the payload types `std::string`, `bytes` and `payload`, with
  `std::allocator`, the user's choice;
* on Emscripten, the C library's `select()`, which Asio's reactor calls on
  every wait of an `io_context` with a timer or a socket, and which callocs
  an array on every call.

The browser's `event_loop` and the test's memory relays are shared by every
room of a page or of a test: they allocate the entries of their
registrations and their relays through the resource they are constructed
with, and a registered callback stays in its own resource, a room's for a
room's object.

[#trystero-allocation-proof]
=== The proof

`test/allocation_test.cpp`, on both targets, counts the global
`operator new`, which it replaces, and the C library's allocation functions,
where the platform lets a program see them: glibc's are replaced and forward to
`+__libc_*+`, Emscripten's dlmalloc has weak definitions the test replaces,
and macOS reports each to `malloc_logger`; MSVC's are not counted, nor any
under AddressSanitizer, whose own allocator reports to none of these. Only
the thread `main` runs on is counted.

* Two rooms over the memory relays exchange a payload, a
  `std::pmr::string`, a `pmr_bytes` and a `boost::json::value` in turn,
  while both counts see none and the counting resources of the two
  configurations see the rooms'.
* Two rooms with a password, a log sink and the relays' state in a resource
  of its own are made, join, exchange a payload that travels in chunks,
  measure a round trip, one says goodbye, both are destroyed and the
  `io_context` is drained. From the first construction to the drain,
  `operator new` sees exactly the two rooms' JSON storage counters and the C
  library nothing. Once the rooms are destroyed their resources still have
  memory out, the operations they left pending, and none once the
  `io_context` has drained.

The counting resources also count a return of memory they never gave, a
`std::pmr` object freed through the wrong resource, which must be none.
Asio's once-per-context allocations are made before anything is counted;
without them the count is 8, not 2. On Emscripten the test also replaces
`select()` with one that keeps its array on the stack, so that the count
sees the rooms' allocations rather than the C library's wait. The test is
declared in `test/Jamfile`:
