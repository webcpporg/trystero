[#trystero-allocation]
== Allocation

A webcpp library lets its user choose where its allocations come from.
trystero does it through `std::pmr`, header-only, with an app resource, an
optional resource for each room, and a resource for the platform backend's
own state.

[#trystero-allocation-resource]
=== The app's resource, the room's and the backend's

* `app_config::memory`, `std::pmr::get_default_resource()` by default,
  receives the app's state, configuration copy including its password, self id,
  registry, shared
  connections, advertised tokens, kept frames, drain order and presence
  frames. Peer connections, data channels and received frames use it even
  while a room negotiates them. The logger, sink and every sentence of the
  app and its rooms use this resource too. It must be non-null, checked by
  `BOOST_ASSERT` at app construction.
* `room_options::memory`, the app's resource when null, receives the room's
  state, bindings and their drain waiters, negotiation records, rebinding
  counts and backoffs, sealed texts, relays, actions, queues, outgoing
  frames, transfers, the documents of the JSON texts it reads, and handlers.
  The signing context is made there with
  `secp256k1_context_preallocated_create`.
* `platform::backend_config::memory`, `std::pmr::get_default_resource()` by
  default, receives the platform backend's own state: its copy of
  `bind_address`, its window of constructions and its own webcpp::webrtc
  runtime. It must be non-null, and outlive the backend and every connection
  of its runtime. What the backend makes for an app or a room is in the
  resource given with that call instead (<<trystero-allocation-asio>>).
* Adoption replaces negotiation handlers with the app's; ending a
  negotiation clears the room's handlers. Detachment fails and frees a
  binding's drain waiters and removes its registrations before returning.
  After a room ends, the app retains no allocation in that room's resource.
* A received frame starts in the app's resource. A `pmr_bytes` payload
  moves from its message buffer when that buffer and the room use the same
  resource; otherwise it is copied once into the room's resource. A chunked
  transfer assembles its buffer in the room's resource. Received
  `std::pmr::string` text is always copied once into the room's resource.
  A JSON text is parsed into a `webcpp::document::pmr::document` of the
  room's resource, a remote candidate into one of the app's, and a JSON
  payload received as a `webcpp::document::pmr::document` into one of the
  room's resource: the document's image, which holds every value, strings
  included, and the working state of the parse that made it.

All resource access runs on the app's executor. A resource need not be
thread-safe when that executor runs on one thread; several `io_context`
threads require a strand.

The allocation test builds every part of `app_config` in its counted
resource before the counting window, including strings, relay URLs, ICE
servers and log sink; `app_config_in` and `options_in` name both resources:

[#trystero-allocation-lifetime]
=== How long the resource lives

The app's resource must outlive its state: the app handle, every room of
it, retained values allocated there and every pending operation. A room's
own resource must outlive that room, its retained `std::pmr::string`,
`pmr_bytes` and `webcpp::document::pmr::document` values, and its pending
operations.
The backend's resource must outlive the backend and every connection of its
own runtime, which a handler the `io_context` still holds may keep alive.
Destroying a handle closes its work but queued completions can still free
memory. Declare the `backend_config::memory`, `app_config::memory` and
`room_options::memory` resources before the `io_context`, then construct the
backend, app and rooms. Drain or destroy the `io_context` before destroying
any of these resources.
In the browser, keep them until all pending promises settle: a pending
`async_await` holds its handler, so in practice resources live for the page's
life.

No check in debug builds catches the misuse: trystero holds a raw pointer
and cannot see the resource end, and the use comes after the room is gone,
from an operation of Asio's or of the browser's. A resource that asserts
nothing is out when it is destroyed, as the test's counting resources do, is
the check, and it is the user's.

A room allocates and frees on every event, a frame, a parsed text, a post or
a log line, so a resource that reuses what is freed, a pool such as
`std::pmr::unsynchronized_pool_resource`, suits a long-lived room; a
`std::pmr::monotonic_buffer_resource` grows with every event and suits a
short-lived one, or a test.

[#trystero-allocation-completions]
=== Completion handlers

A completion handler is the user's, and is allocated as its own associated
allocator says: the room never binds its allocator over it.
`webcpp::asio::bind_allocator` gives one the room's resource. The allocation
test makes the allocator of a resource:

[source]
----
include::{library-root}/test/allocation_test.cpp[tag=allocator,indent=0]
----

and binds it over the completions of a transfer and of a ping, so that they
too are allocated in the sender's resource:

[source]
----
include::{library-root}/test/allocation_test.cpp[tag=bind-allocator,indent=0]
----

When the room erases a completion handler's type (an
`any_completion_handler`), it records whether the handler's associated
executor is the room's, which only the concrete type tells without
allocating; such a completion runs from an operation the room posts to its
executor, allocated with the handler's allocator. One bound to another
executor is handed to that executor by Asio, whose type-erased executor wraps
it with `std::allocator`.

[#trystero-allocation-containers]
=== Containers and shared objects

Every container trystero declares is a `std::pmr` one or a Boost container
over `std::pmr::polymorphic_allocator`: `std::pmr::string` (and `peer_id`),
`std::pmr::vector` (and `backend::bytes`, `protocol::bytes` and
`pmr_bytes`), `std::pmr::deque`, `std::pmr::map` and `std::pmr::set`, and
`boost::unordered_flat_set` (the event ids a strategy remembers),
`boost::circular_buffer` (the construction window, the recent challenges)
and `boost::circular_buffer_space_optimized` (the replay cache), built over
their owner's resource; `app_config`, `room_options`, `send_options`,
`message_info` and `backend::ice_server` hold them too. Two types stay the
user's choice of `std::allocator`: `trystero::bytes`, a
`std::vector<std::uint8_t>`, and `trystero::payload`, a variant of
`std::string`, `webcpp::document::document` and `bytes`. A received
`bytes`, or the binary of a `payload`, is a copy of the frame, which starts
in the app's resource; a received `webcpp::document::document` is in the
standard allocator, its image and the working state of its parse, as
document's `document` is. `webcpp::document::pmr::document` is document's
own allocator-aware type, over `std::pmr::polymorphic_allocator<std::byte>`,
which receives in the room's resource.

A struct that holds `std::pmr` containers takes the room's
`std::pmr::polymorphic_allocator<>` in its constructor, and a container of
such structs is given it when it makes one, with `try_emplace(key,
allocator)`. A `std::pmr` container is never copied without an allocator:
its copy constructor takes the default resource, so a copy names the
resource, `std::pmr::string(text, allocator)`. Moving keeps the resource.

Shared objects are made with `std::allocate_shared` over
`std::pmr::polymorphic_allocator<>`, never with `std::make_shared`,
`std::make_unique` or `new`; a timer is a `std::shared_ptr` for that reason.

No type of trystero is a data structure meant for a shared segment, one that
a process builds there and another maps and uses in place: every type is a
run-time object, an app, a room, an action, a socket, a handler or the
backend, routed through the `std::pmr` resources above, or a value one takes
or gives. A `std::pmr` container holds its resource and its storage at
absolute addresses of its process, as a handler holds a callable, so none is
shareable in the sense of Boost.Interprocess, and none takes an `Allocator`
template parameter for it.

[#trystero-allocation-handlers]
=== Handlers

`std::function` takes no allocator since {cpp}17, so trystero holds every
handler it keeps in `+handler<R(Args...)>+`, a move-only callable that
allocates through a `std::pmr::polymorphic_allocator<>`. Constructed with
`handler(std::allocator_arg, allocator, f)`, it holds `f` inline when `f`
fits in three pointers and is nothrow-movable, and in storage of the
allocator otherwise. It replaces every `std::function` of the backend
interface (the handlers of its timer, WebSocket, data channel, peer
connection and sealer), of the room and of the log's sink. Constructed from
`f` alone it uses the default resource; the app moves `app_config::log`
into its resource with the allocator-extended move.
A move assignment carries the source's allocator with its target.

[#trystero-allocation-asio]
=== Asio, the backends, webcpp::document and the protocol

*Asio,* webcpp::asio's, which natively is Boost.Asio itself. Every `post`,
`dispatch`, timer wait and `any_completion_handler` the room makes carries
`webcpp::asio::bind_allocator` over its resource. A post through
`webcpp::asio::any_io_executor` is wrapped by Asio in a function
object allocated with `std::allocator`, from Asio's per-thread recycling
cache, which calls `std::aligned_alloc` on a miss (measured: two per post,
in every round of five posts, natively and on emscripten). So the room, and
the platform backend's sealer, post through the executor of an `io_context`
when the executor they were given is one, where Asio allocates the operation
as the handler's allocator says, and in a page the sealer awaits each Web
Crypto promise through it too, so that its settlement is posted the same way.
On another executor, a strand over the `io_context` for one, the wrapper
stays Asio's (<<trystero-allocation-outside>>). The executor is the
program's: the examples and the tests give the app and the backend the
executor of their `io_context`.

*The backends.* Each method of `backend::factory` that makes an object takes
a `std::pmr::memory_resource*` as its last parameter. Peer connections,
data channels and received frames use the app's resource; relay sockets,
sealers and room timers use the room's. Objects and their handlers allocate
through that supplied resource. The backend's WebRTC
(<<trystero-design-backend-webrtc>>) gives webcpp::webrtc's connections and
channels a `std::pmr::polymorphic_allocator` on the app's resource, which
their state, their queues, their texts and their operations use: each
operation's handler is bound to it. The platform backend's relays are one
session of webcpp::beast's on both targets, made in the room's resource with
its handlers, its queued texts and its read buffer, and each of its
operations' handlers carries the room's allocator, so that what Asio and
Beast allocate for them is there too. Natively Boost.Beast's stream state
stays Beast's (<<trystero-allocation-outside>>); in a page the WebSocket
stream's own state is in the room's resource too, since the stream is made
with `std::allocator_arg` and the room's allocator. The
platform backend's own state, its copy of `bind_address`, its window of
constructions and its own runtime, is in `backend_config::memory`
(<<trystero-allocation-resource>>): the test `platform_backend`, on both
targets, makes a backend while a counting resource is the default one, which
sees none of it, and sees `backend_config::memory` given all of it back once
the backend is destroyed.

*webcpp::document* is allocator-aware: a `webcpp::document::pmr::document`
allocates through a `std::pmr::polymorphic_allocator<std::byte>` its image,
one buffer in which Boost.Interprocess's `rbtree_best_fit` heap holds every
value, strings included, with offsets for pointers, and the working state of
what reads or writes it: the parser's state, the path and the pending key
and text of the value being read, the tree's record of each change, and the
writer's stack, each freed before the call that made it returns. trystero
reads each text with `webcpp::document::parse(text, options, allocator)`
into a document of the room's resource, of the app's for a remote
candidate: its image starts at 4 KiB and grows in place, twice as large
each time, up to twice the caller's bound and 64 KiB; a text given whole
leaves Boost.JSON's parser no state to suspend. A text the room writes is
appended field by field to a `std::pmr::string` of its resource, its
strings quoted by `webcpp::document::quote`, which allocates only through
that string, and a JSON payload is written by
`webcpp::document::stringify_to` in pieces of 8 bytes, which no standard
library's string keeps outside its own object, its writer's stack in the
document's own allocator.

*The protocol* (`protocol/`) takes a `std::pmr::memory_resource*` as the
last parameter of every function that returns what it allocates, with the
default resource as its default, and returns `std::pmr` types.
`protocol::nostr::verify` uses `secp256k1_context_static` and allocates
nothing.

[#trystero-allocation-outside]
=== What stays outside the resource

* libdatachannel's and OpenSSL's own allocations, the copy libdatachannel's C
  API makes of a message before webcpp::webrtc reads it into the frame, and,
  natively, webcpp::webrtc's copies of what libdatachannel's threads report,
  in the standard allocator;
* what `emscripten::val` holds, in the JavaScript heap;
* Asio's own once-per-context allocations: its services, in a page
  webcpp::asio's bridge among them, and the scheduler's first operation;
* the wrapper Asio puts around a post through an executor that is not the
  executor of an `io_context`, a strand for one, and, given such an
  executor, around the settlement of each Web Crypto promise of a page's
  sealer;
* webcpp::webrtc's posts from libdatachannel's threads, which carry Asio's
  default allocator, since the resource is touched only on the room's
  executor; and the wrapper Asio puts around webcpp::webrtc's completions,
  which it posts through `webcpp::asio::any_io_executor`;
* natively, for each relay connection, what Boost.Beast and Asio allocate of
  their own, which takes no allocator from a handler: the state of the
  WebSocket stream and of the TCP stream under it, the WebSocket's write
  buffer, made at its first write, and, over TLS, the two 17 KiB buffers of
  Asio's TLS stream; the resolver's state and its results, with what the C
  library's `getaddrinfo` allocates on Asio's resolver thread; the WebSocket
  handshake's request and response, the header fields of both and, for a
  response that is not 101, its body, which Beast's own parser reads up to
  Beast's default limit for a response, 8 MiB, that no option of its stream
  lowers (measured: an 8 MiB body held about 18 MiB at its peak, as its
  string grew); over TLS, the verify callback Asio's `set_verify_callback`
  makes with `new`, with the copy of the host that `host_name_verification`
  holds, a `std::string`; each wait of Beast's timers, from Asio's
  per-thread recycling cache, which calls `std::aligned_alloc` on a miss:
  the TCP stream's deadline over the connection and the TLS handshake, and
  the WebSocket's handshake, idle and close timeouts; and each keep-alive
  ping's frame, with `new`. The WebSocket's operations, its read buffer and
  its queued texts are in the room's resource on both targets, since each of
  its handlers carries the room's allocator, and a message allocates nothing
  outside it (measured with a stack for each `operator new` on the room's
  thread: a hundred texts sent and received made none but the write
  buffer's, at the first); in a page the stream's own state is there too,
  and the page's `WebSocket` makes the handshake;
* the payload types `std::string`, `bytes`, `webcpp::document::document` and
  `payload`, with `std::allocator`, the user's choice;
* on Emscripten, the C library's `select()`, which Asio's reactor calls on
  every wait of an `io_context` with a timer or a socket, and which callocs
  an array on every call;
* on Emscripten under Asyncify, the unwind data Asyncify allocates whenever
  the browser program's `run` suspends;
* in the browser, the storage of the map in which webcpp::asio's bridge finds
  each pending Web Crypto promise, in the allocator of the `io_context`,
  made for the first and grown when more are pending at once than it holds,
  and kept until the `io_context` is destroyed. The rest of a page's seal or
  open, the wait for its promise and the post of its settlement, is in the
  room's resource when the backend's executor is that of an `io_context`: the
  test `platform_page_sealer_allocation`, after a first seal and open, sees
  neither `operator new` nor the C library allocate from the start of a seal
  or an open to its completion. The registrations of a WebSocket and of a
  `page_call` are intrusive, the events of a socket are posted in the room's
  resource, and the calls of a `page_call` in the resource it is given.

The test's memory relays are shared by every room of a test: they allocate
their relays through the resource they are constructed with.

[#trystero-allocation-proof]
=== The proof

`test/allocation_test.cpp`, on both targets, counts the global
`operator new`, which it replaces, and the C library's allocation functions,
where the platform lets a program see them: glibc's are replaced and forward to
`+__libc_*+`, Emscripten's dlmalloc has weak definitions the test replaces,
and macOS reports each to `malloc_logger`; MSVC's are not counted, nor any
under AddressSanitizer, whose own allocator reports to none of these. Only
the thread `main` runs on is counted, and every global allocation it counts
is kept, by its address, in a table of a fixed size until it is freed.

* Two rooms over the memory relays exchange a payload, a
  `std::pmr::string`, a `pmr_bytes` and a `webcpp::document::pmr::document`
  in turn, the document written by the sender and parsed by the receiver:
  neither `operator new` nor the C library sees an allocation, none is out
  when the window closes, and the counting resources of the two
  configurations see the rooms'. The same exchange, with a handler that
  allocates outside the resources, an `operator new` it leaves out until the
  window closes and a call of the C library, sees exactly those: the zeros
  are not for want of counting.
* Each text a room reads, a relay's event carrying an offer, a relay's
  `OK`, a signal, a handshake message and a candidate, is parsed into a
  document of a counting resource: neither `operator new` nor the C library
  sees an allocation, and the image goes back to the resource with the
  document.
* Two rooms with a password, a log sink and the relays' state in a resource
  of its own are made, join, exchange a payload that travels in chunks,
  measure a round trip, one says goodbye, both are destroyed and the
  `io_context` is drained. From the first construction to the drain, while
  the rooms and the relay read and write their JSON texts, neither
  `operator new` nor the C library sees an allocation. Once the rooms are
  destroyed their resources still have memory out, the operations they left
  pending, and none once the `io_context` has drained.
* Two apps with two rooms each exchange binary payloads, one room using the
  app's resource and one its own. Size-specific counters prove one payload
  copy into the distinct room resource, and no extra copy when resources
  match. From construction through the drain neither `operator new` nor the
  C library sees an allocation, and every resource ends with nothing out.

The counting resources also count a return of memory they never gave, a
`std::pmr` object freed through the wrong resource, which must be none.
Asio's once-per-context allocations are made before anything is counted;
without them, the 6 they make would still be out at the drain. On
Emscripten the test also replaces `select()` with one that keeps its array
on the stack, so that the count
sees the rooms' allocations rather than the C library's wait; and, under
Asyncify, where the test's own `run_for` suspends, it marks Asyncify's
allocation of a suspension's unwind data and counts it apart, as
webcpp::asio's allocation test does. The test runs in both of
webcpp::asio's suspension variants. It is declared in `test/Jamfile`:
